## [Unreleased]

### Added

#### Resilience — a named policy around every call to a dependency

`cargo add nest-rs --features resilience` brings `nest_rs::resilience`: a
`ResiliencePolicy<I>` per dependency an app calls, named by an `#[instance]`
marker and built by `ResilienceModule::<I>`, which the module whose provider
injects it imports bare (`<PREFIX>_RESILIENCE__<INSTANCE>__*`, or
`ResilienceModule::for_root((Marker, ResilienceConfig { .. }))` to pin a base).
Every module importing one instance shares its one policy, so one breaker counts
the failures of every caller. ([Resilience](https://nestrs.dev/fundamentals/resilience/))

- **Every wait is bounded.** Each attempt is given up past `TIMEOUT_MS` and its
  `CancellationToken` cancelled; a retry waits `nest_rs_core::Backoff`'s jittered
  curve, from `BACKOFF_FIRST_MS` up to `BACKOFF_CEILING_MS`; a call waits
  `CONCURRENCY_WAIT_MS` at most for a slot of the bulkhead (`CONCURRENCY`).
- **A call is attempted again only where the caller declares it idempotent**:
  `run` sends it once, `run_idempotent` again after a failure or a timeout, up to
  `RETRIES`. Every `Err` counts as the dependency failing; an answer the
  dependency gave belongs in the call's `Ok`.
- **A circuit breaker** counts the latest `BREAKER_WINDOW` attempts, opens once
  `BREAKER_FAILURES` of them failed, refuses every call unsent for
  `BREAKER_OPEN_SECS`, then lets one probe through. Each transition is a `warn`
  on `nest_rs::resilience` and, beside the `opentelemetry` feature, one more on
  the counter `nestrs.resilience.breaker.transitions`, by
  `nestrs.resilience.instance` and `nestrs.resilience.breaker.state`.
- **A refusal is answered `503`, `UNAVAILABLE`, on every edge**: a timeout, an
  open breaker — with the `Retry-After` it knows — and a full bulkhead, as a
  `ResilienceError` implementing `ToProblem`, whose problem rides its chain, so
  a queue job failing with it waits at least that long.
- **`ResilienceMiddleware`, beside the `reqwest` feature,** runs the app's own
  reqwest client under a policy: it sends again what its method (RFC 9110
  §9.2.2's, and `QUERY`) or an `Idempotency-Key` declares idempotent, on a
  transport failure, a timeout or a `408`, `429`, `500`, `502`, `503` or `504`,
  honouring a `Retry-After` up to the backoff's ceiling, in seconds or as an
  HTTP-date. Added before `ReqwestMiddleware`, each attempt is a client span of
  its own carrying `http.request.resend_count`; added after it, it refuses every
  request, unsent, saying so.
- **No attempt outlives its unit**: each ends at the policy's timeout or the
  unit's deadline, whichever comes first, and no retry starts past that
  deadline. A failed call answers as its error's `ToProblem` says, and a
  `Retry-After` the error's chain carries holds its retry back.
- **A policy's state lives in its process**, each replica's breaker and bulkhead
  its own, and the page says so.
- **Every policy counts its retries and the refusals its runs end on**, beside
  its breaker's transitions: `nestrs.resilience.retries` and
  `nestrs.resilience.refusals`, by `nestrs.resilience.refusal`. A call waits for
  a slot of the bulkhead within the unit's deadline, never past it.

Thanks to Just-Replicant, whose resilience proposal (#7) brought the bulkhead's
wait bounded by the call's deadline and the counters of retries and refusals,
both carried into this one.

#### A unit's deadline is ambient on every edge

`nest_rs::core::Deadline` — the instant a unit ends by — is ambient in every
unit its edge cuts at a deadline: an HTTP request, a WebSocket message, a
GraphQL or MCP operation, a job attempt, a tick, a listener call. Work inside
can stop waiting before the cut; a unit nested in another ends by the sooner.

#### `HttpsPeer`, the server an HTTP client under test calls

`nest_rs::testing::HttpsPeer` (`tls`) is a loopback HTTPS peer presenting a
`TestAuthority` certificate, answering each request from a script of
`PeerAnswer`s and keeping each `RequestHead`.

`reqwest-middleware` is now public in `nest-rs-resilience` too, at the `0.5`
`nest-rs-reqwest` already holds for 7.x.

### Fixed

- The JWK Set fetcher and the OAuth client read a `Retry-After` in both forms
  RFC 9110 gives it, an HTTP-date counted from the answer's own `Date`, where
  each read only its seconds; the OAuth client no longer takes `+5`, and reads
  a value past what fits as the longest wait rather than none.
