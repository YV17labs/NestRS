A [resilience policy](/fundamentals/resilience/) bounds the load it puts on a
dependency two ways. Its bulkhead caps the calls running inside it at once, and
its rate limit the attempts it sends, a retry included, as a bucket of tokens. Both are off
by default, and each replica keeps its own.

```bash title=".env"
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__CONCURRENCY=16
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__CONCURRENCY_WAIT_MS=500
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__CONCURRENCY_WAITERS=64
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__RATE_LIMIT=100
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__RATE_WINDOW_SECS=60
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__RATE_WAIT_MS=2000
```

Sixteen calls run at once, and a call finding every slot taken waits up to half a
second for one, sixty-four calls waiting at most. The policy holds a hundred
tokens and gains one every 600 ms. An attempt finding none waits for its own
token when it comes within two seconds and before the unit's deadline, and is
refused at once otherwise.

## The bulkhead

A call takes one of the `CONCURRENCY` slots for its whole run — its retries and
every wait included — and gives it back as the run ends. A call finding every
slot taken waits up to `CONCURRENCY_WAIT_MS` for one, or what is left of the
unit's deadline when that is sooner. It is refused `Full`, unsent, when none is
freed in time. With no wait, the default, it is refused at once.

`CONCURRENCY_WAITERS` bounds how many calls wait at once: a call finding every
slot taken and as many calls waiting is refused `Full` at once. A waiter gives
its place up as it takes a slot, runs out of its wait, or is dropped by its
caller. The bound means nothing without `CONCURRENCY`, nor beside a wait of
none, where no call waits.

Before it takes a slot, a run asks the unit's deadline, then the breaker. A unit
out of time is refused `TimedOut` and a call meeting an open breaker `Open`, at
once, never after a wait for a slot. A run finding no free slot reads the rate
too. When the token due it, behind the attempts already waiting for theirs, lies
past `CONCURRENCY_WAIT_MS` plus `RATE_WAIT_MS` from its arrival, or at the unit's
deadline or after it, the call is refused `Limited` at once rather than after
its wait. That read takes nothing and is advisory: any other call waits for its
slot and meets the rate as it stands once it holds one, so it may wait for a slot
and be refused by the rate after. And a call it refuses might have been served
by a token another attempt gave back since.

To see the bulkhead, read the policy: `concurrency_running()` is the calls
holding a slot — through `ResilienceMiddleware`, until the response's body
ends — and `concurrency_waiting()` the calls waiting for one, each `None`
for a policy with no bulkhead. A call counts as running from the moment its own
future holds the slot: a waiter handed a freed slot reads as waiting until it
runs, so a call is in one of the two at any instant, never both. With OpenTelemetry on, they are the
`nestrs.resilience.concurrency.running` and
`nestrs.resilience.concurrency.waiting` instruments
([the metrics](/opentelemetry/metrics/#resilience-policies)), the pair an
alert on a saturated dependency reads.

A call the bulkhead refuses files `bulkhead full; the call is refused` at
`debug` on `nest_rs::resilience`, its `host` and `method` from a decorated
method. When every place to wait was taken, its `waiters` is the bound it met.

## The rate limit

`RATE_LIMIT` counts every attempt the policy sends, a retry included, whatever
runs it: a `#[resilient]` method, `run`, `run_idempotent` or
`ResilienceMiddleware`. An attempt asks, in order, what is left of the unit's
deadline, the breaker, then the rate, and only then is its call sent. So a call
an open breaker refuses counts nothing. An attempt past the limit is not sent
and settles nothing in the breaker, a probe handing its turn to the next call.

The rate is a token bucket. It holds `RATE_LIMIT` tokens, full at boot, and
gains one every `RATE_WINDOW_SECS / RATE_LIMIT`, never more than `RATE_LIMIT`.
Each attempt takes a token to be sent. One finding none free is not sent: it is
refused `Limited`, its `Retry-After` the time to the token due it, the next one
no attempt already waiting is due, unless `RATE_WAIT_MS` reaches that token
before the unit's deadline, and it waits for it instead. It then asks the deadline and the breaker again, and is sent unless one
refuses it. It waits once, the time it is told as it reserves its token, or less.
Its slot of the bulkhead stays held while it waits. The wait is not the
attempt's: its timeout starts when its call is sent, and the unit's deadline
bounds the wait and the attempt together.

**Attempts waiting are served in the order they arrived.** Each reserves the token
after the last one's, and one arriving stands behind them all. A token past
`RATE_WAIT_MS`, or at the unit's deadline or after it, is not waited for, so no
more attempts wait at once than tokens come within the wait. An attempt given up
while it waits — its call dropped, the breaker refusing it once its wait ends, or
its unit out of time by then — gives its token to the attempt behind it, and
every attempt behind moves up one.

**`Limited` ends the run.** A retry the rate refuses is not attempted again, so
a limit leaving a run's retries no token within the wait loses them.

<Aside type="caution">
Each replica counts its own attempts. A limit your dependency sets for the whole
app is its quota divided by the replicas sharing it: a hundred calls a minute
across four replicas is `RATE_LIMIT=25`.
</Aside>

**A bucket left idle for a window is full.** It sends `RATE_LIMIT` attempts at
once, then a token at a time: up to twice the limit goes out within one window's
length after an idle spell. A dependency counting every request of the last
window is given half the limit it allows: set `RATE_LIMIT` to half its share per
replica.

**A token keeps its instant when its attempt is woken late.** A runtime that wakes
an attempt after its token's instant sends it then, so two attempts sent after a
wait can go out closer together than a token's interval, by that lag.

A call the rate refuses files `rate limit reached; the call is refused` at
`debug`, its `retry_after_ms` the time to the token due it. One waiting for its
token files `rate limit reached; waiting for a token`, a `debug` too, its
`wait_ms` that time. Each names its `host` and `method` from a decorated method,
every duration in whole milliseconds rounded up, never `0` for a wait. A policy
with no `RATE_LIMIT` builds no rate: an attempt takes no lock, allocates nothing
and arms no timer for it.

`rate_available()` reads the tokens free now: what as many attempts arriving
together would take, none while an attempt waits for one, `None` for a policy
with no rate limit. With OpenTelemetry on, it is the
`nestrs.resilience.rate.available` instrument
([the metrics](/opentelemetry/metrics/#resilience-policies)).

### What an attempt is

The rate counts attempts, not the requests an attempt makes. A redirect your
reqwest client follows goes out inside the attempt that met it: under its
timeout, judged by the answer it ends on, and counted once. So does a second
request in one decorated body or `run` closure. Where your dependency counts
every request it is sent, make each attempt send one. Build its client with
`reqwest::redirect::Policy::none()`: the redirect is then the attempt's answer,
and the request after it an attempt of its own. Send each request from a
decorated method or a `run` closure of its own.

## Configuration

Each key reads as [Resilience](/fundamentals/resilience/#configuration) says,
under the policy's instance.

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `CONCURRENCY` | how many calls run inside the policy at once, the bulkhead — default none; from `1` to `65536`, `0` for none |
| `CONCURRENCY_WAIT_MS` | how long a call waits for a free slot before it is refused, the unit's deadline permitting — default `0`, no wait: refused at once; else from `1` to `60000` (a minute) |
| `CONCURRENCY_WAITERS` | how many calls wait for a slot at once — default none; from `1` to `65536`, `0` for none |
| `RATE_LIMIT` | how many tokens the bucket holds, the attempts the policy sends over a window, a retry included — default none; from `1` to `1000000000`, `0` for none |
| `RATE_WINDOW_SECS` | the time an empty bucket takes to fill, a token back every `RATE_WINDOW_SECS / RATE_LIMIT` — default `60`; from `1` to `86400` (a day), and in code anything above zero up to a day |
| `RATE_WAIT_MS` | the longest one attempt waits for its token; a token further off, or at the unit's deadline or after it, is not waited for, the attempt refused at once — default `0`, no wait: refused at once; else from `1` to `60000` (a minute) |

The wait and the waiters mean nothing without `CONCURRENCY`, and the window and
its wait nothing without `RATE_LIMIT`: each builds nothing on its own.

## Limits

- **The bounds are each replica's.** A replica's bulkhead bounds the calls it
  runs and the callers it holds waiting, and its rate counts the attempts it
  sent.
- **A wait for a token holds its slot of the bulkhead**, so a call waiting on
  the rate keeps another out of the bulkhead for as long.
- **The rate read before a wait for a slot is advisory.** It refuses at once
  only a call whose token lies past `CONCURRENCY_WAIT_MS` plus `RATE_WAIT_MS`
  from its arrival, or at the unit's deadline or after it: another may wait for
  its slot and then be refused by the rate, and one refused at once might have
  been served by a token given back since.
- **A bucket left idle sends up to twice the limit within a window's length**,
  and a woken-late attempt goes out closer to the one before it than a token's
  interval.
- **A redirect or a second request in one body is no attempt of its own**, so a
  dependency counting every request can be sent more than `RATE_LIMIT` in a
  window ([what an attempt is](#what-an-attempt-is)).
- **`RATE_LIMIT` is the bucket's depth as well as its rate**, and the attempts
  waiting for a token are bounded by the tokens due within `RATE_WAIT_MS`: no
  setting sizes either apart.
