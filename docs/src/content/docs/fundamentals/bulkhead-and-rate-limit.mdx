A [resilience policy](/fundamentals/resilience/) bounds the load it puts on a
dependency two ways. Its bulkhead caps the calls running inside it at once, and
its rate limit the attempts it sends per window, a retry included. Both are off
by default, and each replica keeps its own.

```bash title=".env"
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__CONCURRENCY=16
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__CONCURRENCY_WAIT_MS=500
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__CONCURRENCY_WAITERS=64
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__RATE_LIMIT=100
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__RATE_WINDOW_SECS=60
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__RATE_WAIT_MS=2000
```

Sixteen calls run at once, and a call finding every slot taken waits up to half a
second for one, sixty-four calls waiting at most. The policy sends a hundred
attempts a minute. An attempt past them waits for the window's reset when that
comes within two seconds and before the unit's deadline, and is refused at once
otherwise.

## The bulkhead

A call takes one of the `CONCURRENCY` slots for its whole run — its retries and
every wait included — and gives it back as the run ends. A call finding every
slot taken waits up to `CONCURRENCY_WAIT_MS` for one, or what is left of the
unit's deadline when that is sooner. It is refused `Full`, unsent, when none is
freed in time. With no wait, the default, it is refused at once.

`CONCURRENCY_WAITERS` bounds how many calls wait at once: a call finding every
slot taken and as many calls waiting is refused `Full` at once. A waiter gives
its place up as it takes a slot, runs out of its wait, or is dropped by its
caller. The bound means nothing without `CONCURRENCY`, nor beside a wait of
none, where no call waits.

Before it takes a slot, a run asks the unit's deadline, then the breaker. A unit
out of time is refused `TimedOut` and a call meeting an open breaker `Open`, at
once, never after a wait for a slot. A run finding no free slot reads the rate
too. Once the window is spent and its reset lies past `RATE_WAIT_MS`, or at the
unit's deadline or after it, the call is refused `Limited` at once rather than
after its wait. That read counts nothing and is advisory. A window others spend
after it still costs the call its wait for a slot, and its attempt then meets
the rate as any does.

A call the bulkhead refuses files `bulkhead full; the call is refused` at
`debug` on `nest_rs::resilience`, its `host` and `method` from a decorated
method. When every place to wait was taken, its `waiters` is the bound it met.

## The rate limit

`RATE_LIMIT` counts every attempt the policy sends, a retry included, whatever
runs it: a `#[resilient]` method, `run`, `run_idempotent` or
`ResilienceMiddleware`. An attempt asks, in order, what is left of the unit's
deadline, the breaker, then the rate, and only then is its call sent. So a call
an open breaker refuses counts nothing. An attempt past the limit is not sent
and settles nothing in the breaker, a probe handing its turn to the next call.

The window is fixed, as the throttler's stores count one: the first attempt
counted opens it, and the count starts again once it has passed. Past the limit
an attempt is refused `Limited`, its `Retry-After` the time to the window's
reset. When `RATE_WAIT_MS` reaches that reset before the unit's deadline, the
attempt waits for it instead, then asks the deadline, the breaker and the rate
again. The waits of one attempt never add up past `RATE_WAIT_MS`, and a reset it
cannot reach is not waited for. Its slot of the bulkhead stays held while it
waits. The wait is not the attempt's: its timeout starts when its call is sent,
and the unit's deadline bounds the wait and the attempt together.

**`Limited` ends the run.** A retry the rate refuses is not attempted again, so
a limit leaving a run's retries no room in one window loses them, unless
`RATE_WAIT_MS` reaches the reset.

<Aside type="caution">
Each replica counts its own attempts. A limit your dependency sets for the whole
app is its quota divided by the replicas sharing it: a hundred calls a minute
across four replicas is `RATE_LIMIT=25`.
</Aside>

**A fixed window lets up to twice the limit through around a reset**: one
window's last attempts and the next window's first fall within one window's
length. A dependency counting over a sliding window is given half its quota: its
share per replica, halved.

A call the rate refuses files `rate limit reached; the call is refused` at
`debug`, its `retry_after_ms` the time to the reset. One waiting for the reset
files `rate limit reached; waiting for the window to reset`, a `debug` too, its
`wait_ms` that time. Each names its `host` and `method` from a decorated method,
every duration in whole milliseconds rounded up. A policy with no `RATE_LIMIT`
builds no rate: an attempt takes no lock, allocates nothing and arms no timer
for it.

### What an attempt is

The rate counts attempts, not the requests an attempt makes. A redirect your
reqwest client follows goes out inside the attempt that met it: under its
timeout, judged by the answer it ends on, and counted once. So does a second
request in one decorated body or `run` closure. Where your dependency counts
every request it is sent, make each attempt send one. Build its client with
`reqwest::redirect::Policy::none()`: the redirect is then the attempt's answer,
and the request after it an attempt of its own. Send each request from a
decorated method or a `run` closure of its own.

## Configuration

Each key reads as [Resilience](/fundamentals/resilience/#configuration) says,
under the policy's instance.

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `CONCURRENCY` | how many calls run inside the policy at once, the bulkhead — default none; from `1` to `65536`, `0` for none |
| `CONCURRENCY_WAIT_MS` | how long a call waits for a free slot before it is refused, the unit's deadline permitting — default `0`, no wait: refused at once; else from `1` to `60000` (a minute) |
| `CONCURRENCY_WAITERS` | how many calls wait for a slot at once — default none; from `1` to `65536`, `0` for none |
| `RATE_LIMIT` | how many attempts the policy sends per window, a retry included — default none; from `1` to `1000000000`, `0` for none |
| `RATE_WINDOW_SECS` | the fixed window the limit counts over — default `60`; from `1` to `86400` (a day), and in code anything above zero up to a day |
| `RATE_WAIT_MS` | the longest one attempt waits in all for the window's reset; a reset further off, or at the unit's deadline or after it, is not waited for, the attempt refused at once — default `0`, no wait: refused at once; else from `1` to `60000` (a minute) |

The wait and the waiters mean nothing without `CONCURRENCY`, and the window and
its wait nothing without `RATE_LIMIT`: each builds nothing on its own.

## Limits

- **The bounds are each replica's.** A replica's bulkhead bounds the calls it
  runs and the callers it holds waiting, and its rate counts the attempts it
  sent.
- **A wait for the window's reset holds its slot of the bulkhead**, so a call
  waiting on the rate keeps another out of the bulkhead for as long.
- **A redirect or a second request in one body is no attempt of its own**, so a
  dependency counting every request can be sent more than `RATE_LIMIT` in a
  window ([what an attempt is](#what-an-attempt-is)).
