A [resilience policy](/fundamentals/resilience/) stops calling a dependency that
keeps failing: its circuit breaker counts the outcome of every attempt and opens
on enough failures. While it is open it refuses every call unsent, then probes
the dependency before it lets calls through again. It is on by default, one per
policy, shared by every caller in the process.

```bash title=".env"
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__BREAKER_WINDOW=20
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__BREAKER_FAILURES=10
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__BREAKER_OPEN_SECS=30
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__BREAKER_AGE_SECS=600
NESTRS_RESILIENCE__UPSTREAM__BREAKER_PROBES=3
```

The first three are the defaults. Here an outcome older than ten minutes no
longer counts, and three probes in a row close the breaker. By default no
outcome ages, and one probe closes it.

## How it opens and closes

The breaker counts the latest `BREAKER_WINDOW` attempts. Once `BREAKER_FAILURES`
of them failed, it opens: every call is refused at once, unsent, for
`BREAKER_OPEN_SECS`. Then one call probes the dependency while the others are
still refused. Its failure opens the breaker again, and its success closes it,
or counts toward the [series of probes](#an-age-and-a-series-of-probes)
`BREAKER_PROBES` asks for.

On the defaults, the tenth failure among the latest twenty attempts opens it.
For thirty seconds each call is refused unsent: its client is answered `503` at
once, its `Retry-After` counting down from `30`, instead of after a five-second
timeout. Then one call probes, and the breaker closes or opens for thirty more.

**A share is written as a count.** "Half the calls failed, after at least ten"
is written `BREAKER_FAILURES=10` and `BREAKER_WINDOW=20`, the defaults: half of
a full window, and never before ten calls failed. Until the window holds twenty
outcomes it still waits for ten failures, which makes it the stricter rule. Ten
calls of which five failed leave it closed, where a rate counted from the tenth
call opens it. A share `p`, never before `m` failures, is `BREAKER_FAILURES=m`
and `BREAKER_WINDOW=m/p`: a quarter, never before five, is `5` of `20`.

**One probe at a time.** While a call holds the probe's turn, every other call
is refused, told what is left of that probe's own bound. The bound is the
probe's timeout, its method's pin included, or what its unit's deadline leaves
when that is sooner. While the probe waits for the rate's reset, it counts from
the reset. Past the bound, a refused call is told a millisecond, the probe being
given up at any moment.

## An age and a series of probes

`BREAKER_AGE_SECS` forgets an outcome once it is older than the age, counted
from its call's answer. The window forgets what is past the age each time it
counts an outcome. So failures one short of opening the breaker, aged past it by
a quiet spell, do not open it with the next. Unset, the window counts its latest
attempts however old.

`BREAKER_PROBES` asks that many probes in a row to succeed before the breaker
closes, one at a time. A failed probe opens it again for its whole open time,
and the series starts over. A probe whose call is cut at its timeout or at its
unit's deadline has failed. A probe whose call is never sent, because the rate
refused it or its unit ran out of time first, counts as neither a success nor a
failure. The same goes for one whose caller drops it before it answers, and the
series keeps its count.

Under a rate limit, an age leaves room for only so many failures to answer
within it of one another, each attempt running ten minutes at most. A
`BREAKER_FAILURES` above that never opens the breaker, so the boot refuses it,
naming `BREAKER_AGE_SECS`, `RATE_LIMIT` and `RATE_WINDOW_SECS`.

## Read it and switch it by hand

`ResiliencePolicy::breaker_state()` answers what a call arriving now meets, a
`BreakerState`. It is `Closed`; `Open { retry_after }`, what is left of the open
time, never zero; or `HalfOpen` once the open time has passed, whether the
probe's turn is free, held or out. A policy with no breaker, `BREAKER_WINDOW=0`,
answers `None`.

```rust title="crates/features/src/posts/upstream.rs"
use std::time::Duration;

use nest_rs::resilience::{BreakerState, ResiliencePolicy};

/// How long the upstream's breaker still refuses every call, while it is open.
pub fn refused_for(upstream: &ResiliencePolicy<Upstream>) -> Option<Duration> {
    match upstream.breaker_state()? {
        BreakerState::Open { retry_after, .. } => Some(retry_after),
        _ => None,
    }
}
```

`open_breaker(duration)` takes the dependency out by hand, for a maintenance
window or an outage you already know of. Every call is refused unsent for
`duration`, counted from the call and replacing any open time the breaker had.
Then the dependency is probed as after any opening. `close_breaker()` sends
every call again. Each forgets the window, and a call let through before it
changes nothing once it answers. A probe answering a success after an opening
closes nothing, and one answering a failure after a closing opens nothing. A
probe waiting for the rate's reset loses its turn.

Each answers `Result<(), BreakerError>`: `Off` for a policy with no breaker, and
`OutOfRange` for an opening `breaker_open` would not take in code, none or past
an hour. A duration out of range is never clamped: the breaker is left as it
was. Each moves this replica's breaker alone.

## What it files

Each transition is a `warn` on `nest_rs::resilience`,
`circuit breaker changed state`, naming the `instance` and the `state` it
entered: `open`, `half_open` or `closed`. Failures opening it add `failed`,
`window` and `open_ms`. A switch by hand adds `manual = true`, and an opening
its `open_ms`, filed even when it enters the state it found. Every duration is
in whole milliseconds, rounded up.

The read and the line answer two questions. The read says `HalfOpen` once the
open time has passed, what a call arriving then meets. The line says `half_open`
only when a probe's call is sent, what the dependency meets. Over a series of
probes, `half_open` is filed as its first probe is sent and nothing between two
probes. A failed probe files `open` with its `failed`, `window` and `open_ms`,
and the last success `closed`. A probe given up before its call is sent files
nothing. One dropped once its call is out files `open` when it was its series'
first, and nothing after a success of the series. Each transition counts on
`nestrs.resilience.breaker.transitions`, by `nestrs.resilience.breaker.state`, a
manual one marked `nestrs.resilience.breaker.manual` ([the
counters](/opentelemetry/metrics/#resilience-policies)).

## Test it

What your client is told while the dependency is down takes one failure, not
ten. The test's root pins the policy to open on the first, and the next call is
refused unsent.

```rust title="crates/features/tests/integration/posts/service.rs"
use features::posts::{PostsModule, PostsService, Upstream};
use nest_rs::core::module;
use nest_rs::resilience::{ResilienceConfig, ResilienceError, ResilienceModule};
use nest_rs::testing::HeadlessApp;

#[module(imports = [
    ResilienceModule::for_root((Upstream, ResilienceConfig {
        retries: None,
        breaker_window: Some(1),
        breaker_failures: 1,
        ..ResilienceConfig::default()
    })),
    PostsModule,
])]
struct OpeningOnOneFailure;

#[tokio::test]
async fn an_open_breaker_refuses_the_call_unsent() -> anyhow::Result<()> {
    let app = HeadlessApp::builder().module::<OpeningOnOneFailure>().build_headless().await?;
    let posts = app.container().get::<PostsService>().expect("provided");
    let nowhere = "https://127.0.0.1:1/";

    assert!(matches!(posts.preview(nowhere).await, Err(ResilienceError::Failed(_))));
    assert!(matches!(posts.preview(nowhere).await, Err(ResilienceError::Open { .. })));
    Ok(())
}
```

Or it takes none: opened by hand, the breaker refuses the call unsent; closed by
hand, it sends the next, which fails where nothing listens. Beside the test
above, on its root, which sends each call once:

```rust title="crates/features/tests/integration/posts/service.rs"
use std::time::Duration;

use nest_rs::resilience::{BreakerState, ResiliencePolicy};

#[tokio::test]
async fn a_preview_is_refused_while_opened_by_hand_and_sent_once_closed() -> anyhow::Result<()> {
    let app = HeadlessApp::builder().module::<OpeningOnOneFailure>().build_headless().await?;
    let posts = app.container().get::<PostsService>().expect("provided");
    let upstream = app.container().get::<ResiliencePolicy<Upstream>>().expect("built");
    let nowhere = "https://127.0.0.1:1/";

    upstream.open_breaker(Duration::from_secs(60))?;
    assert!(matches!(posts.preview(nowhere).await, Err(ResilienceError::Open { .. })));

    upstream.close_breaker()?;
    assert_eq!(upstream.breaker_state(), Some(BreakerState::Closed));
    assert!(matches!(posts.preview(nowhere).await, Err(ResilienceError::Failed(_))));
    Ok(())
}
```

## Configuration

Each key reads as [Resilience](/fundamentals/resilience/#configuration) says,
under the policy's instance.

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `BREAKER_WINDOW` | how many of the latest attempts the breaker counts — default `20`; from `1` to `10000`, `0` for no breaker |
| `BREAKER_FAILURES` | how many failed attempts in the window open it — default `10`; from `1` to `BREAKER_WINDOW` |
| `BREAKER_OPEN_SECS` | how long it refuses every call before one probes — default `30`; from `1` to `3600` (an hour), and in code anything above zero up to an hour |
| `BREAKER_AGE_SECS` | how old an outcome grows, from its call's answer, before it no longer counts — default none; from `1` to `86400` (a day), `0` for none, and in code anything above zero up to a day |
| `BREAKER_PROBES` | how many probes in a row close it — default `1`; from `1` to `100` |

The age and the probes mean nothing without a breaker, and build none. Beside a
value out of its range, the boot refuses more failures than the window holds,
and more than the rate lets answer within the age.

## Limits

- **A breaker is its replica's.** It opens on the failures its own replica saw,
  and a switch by hand moves it alone. Taking a dependency out for the whole app
  switches the breaker of every replica.
- **An age costs memory.** With one set, each outcome the window holds keeps the
  instant it answered beside its byte, 24 bytes on 64-bit Linux, about 240 KB at
  the window's ceiling of ten thousand.
- **The boot's check of an age under a rate counts the longest attempt.** It
  takes each attempt as running up to ten minutes, the timeout's ceiling, which
  a method may pin: a policy whose attempts all end sooner can still be built
  with a breaker that never opens. It takes each timeout as firing on time: a
  runtime stalled past a timeout can let more failures answer within the age
  than the check counts.
- **A transition's line is filed under the breaker's lock**, so no switch comes
  between a transition and its line, and the last line says the state the
  breaker is in. A `tracing` subscriber or a meter that reads or switches that
  breaker from inside the line waits for good; none of the framework's does.
