By default every `Err` a decorated method answers is the dependency failing: the
breaker counts it and an idempotent call sends it again. Two keys say otherwise
for the dependency's own answers, and a fallback answers in place of an error. In
`page`, a missing post's `404` is sent once and counted for no failure:

```rust title="crates/features/src/posts/service.rs"
#[resilient(
    upstream,
    idempotent,
    retry_if = |error| !error.missing(),
    record_if = |error| !error.missing()
)]
pub async fn page(&self, post: Uuid) -> Result<String, ResilienceError<UpstreamError>> {
    let url = format!("{}/previews/{post}", self.config.upstream_url);
    let page = reqwest::get(url).await?.error_for_status()?;
    Ok(page.text().await?)
}
```

## What counts as a failure

`retry_if` says which `Err` is sent again, `record_if` which one is a failure in
the breaker, and the two are independent: one says nothing of the other. A `404`
is the upstream's answer, and `page` answers it as
`Err(ResilienceError::Failed(error))` for its caller to read with
`error.missing()` ([the call's error](/fundamentals/resilience/#what-the-client-is-told));
a `503` is attempted again and counted as a failure. An `Err` that `retry_if`
refuses ends the run and is the method's answer.

A predicate reads `self` and the arguments by reference, and `E` is the call's
own error: a timeout is no `E`, so neither `retry_if` nor `record_if` reads it,
and `fallback_if` reads it as `ResilienceError::Refused` holding
`ResilienceRefusal::TimedOut`. A `retry_if`,
`retry_ok_if` or `record_if` that panics fails its attempt as a panic in the body
does: one failure in the breaker, its savepoint rolled back, and the panic
unwinds from the method. `fallback_if` and the fallback are asked once the run
is over, so a panic in either unwinds from the method and fails nothing more.

**An `Err` that `record_if` refuses is counted in neither direction.** It is no
failure and no success either: a closed breaker's window holds nothing of it,
and a half-open breaker's probe answering it hands its turn to the next call.
While half-open, such answers neither close nor reopen the breaker, which stays
half-open, one call at a time, until an `Ok` or a counted `Err` answers.

`retry_ok_if` asks the same of an `Ok`, for a dependency answering "not ready
yet" as a success: one it takes is sent again, and once the retries are spent the
last `Ok` is the method's answer. A timeout is always a failure in the breaker,
and no predicate keeps an idempotent method from sending it again, but for a call
made inside an attempt of its policy that may still be sent again, which is
[sent once](#limits).

An error whose chain carries a `Problem` with a `Retry-After` — an upstream's
`429` passed on — holds its retry back at least that long. One asking past
`BACKOFF_CEILING_MS` ends the run.

An answer the dependency gave can also be the call's `Ok` — `Ok(None)` for a
page it does not have, `Ok(Err(declined))` for a card it declined — neither
attempted again nor counted as a failure. That is how a call run by hand, which
reads no key, says it.

## A fallback

`fallback = method` names a method of `Self` that answers in place of the error
a run ends on: the call's own, from its last attempt, or the policy's refusal —
a timeout, an open breaker, a full bulkhead, a reached rate limit. It takes the
decorated method's arguments, in order, then that error by value, and answers
what the method answers; an `Err` it answers is the method's. `card` answers no
card for a refusal, files it at `warn` with its chain, and leaves the upstream's
own error to its caller:

```rust title="crates/features/src/posts/service.rs"
#[resilient(
    upstream,
    fallback = no_card,
    fallback_if = |error| matches!(error, ResilienceError::Refused { .. })
)]
pub async fn card(&self, post: Uuid) -> Result<Option<String>, ResilienceError<UpstreamError>> {
    let url = format!("{}/previews/{post}", self.config.upstream_url);
    let page = reqwest::get(url).await?.error_for_status()?;
    Ok(Some(page.text().await?))
}

async fn no_card(
    &self,
    _post: Uuid,
    refused: ResilienceError<UpstreamError>,
) -> Result<Option<String>, ResilienceError<UpstreamError>> {
    let error = nest_rs::core::error_message(&refused);
    tracing::warn!(target: crate::posts::TARGET, %error, "link preview refused; answering no card");
    Ok(None)
}
```

`fallback_if` is asked first; left out, every error is answered, the policy's
refusals included, and an error it refuses is the method's answer. The fallback
runs once the run is over, under no policy: no timeout, no retry, counted in no
breaker. `ResilienceError::Unsettled` never reaches it.

Every attempt lends the arguments the fallback is then handed (one under a
`#[cfg]` is handed under that condition, the fallback declaring it under the
same), so the method
binds each to a plain name, none `mut`, takes no `&mut`, and clones what an
attempt consumes, which rustc answers with `E0382` at the fallback's name. A
handler naming its extractor whole, `id: Path<u32>`, takes it apart in its body;
one binding it by a pattern, `Path(id): Path<u32>`, is refused, a pattern binding
`mut` included, in one error. A fallback is called as a method, so it takes
`&self` or `self: &Arc<Self>`; its type parameters are fixed by its arguments and
its answer, one they leave open being rustc's `E0283`, and a `fallback` written
with type arguments or as a call is refused saying to name the method alone. An argument
the method hands on is read, so one named with a leading `_` is
`clippy::used_underscore_binding`'s at the key: drop the underscore. A
fallback taking other arguments is rustc's `E0308` at the key or `E0061` at the
fallback's name, one naming no method of `Self`, a function of `Self` taking no
`self` included, is its `E0599`, and one naming the method it answers for is
refused. The fallback is always a method, whose signature is
checked against the decorated one's: a closure or a free function answers
through a method that calls it.

A call run by hand has no key, and its `Result` is its fallback: an
`.or_else(…)` on what `run` answers chooses what stands in for a refusal.

## A unit's transaction

Inside a unit holding a transaction — a mutating route's, a job's — each attempt
of a method declaring `idempotent` or a `fallback` runs in a savepoint of it,
whatever its retries, none included, and so does each attempt of
`run_idempotent`. What the body writes through the data layer is kept only when
the method answers that attempt's `Ok`, and rolled back otherwise, an `Ok` sent
again by `retry_ok_if` included: an attempt leaves no write for the next to
repeat, nor beside the error the method answers. The last attempt is rolled back
before a fallback runs, and before the method answers an error `fallback_if`
refused. A method run once with no fallback opens none, nor does any outside a
transaction — a safe route, which runs on the pool: an `Err` there leaves what the
body wrote, as it does with no policy.

A savepoint the database cannot settle ends the method on
`ResilienceError::Unsettled`, attempted no further and handed to no fallback: the
unit cannot commit what the attempt wrote. Another statement of the unit, issued
beside the method, waits until an attempt that sent a statement has settled; a
method's future dropped mid-attempt, once the attempt's savepoint was answered,
leaves what holds that savepoint unable to commit, as an
[atomic block](/database/transactions/#atomic-blocks) dropped does: its unit
or, for a method nested in another attempt's savepoint or in an atomic block,
that savepoint or that block alone, which can only roll back while the unit goes
on to commit what it wrote beside it. Dropped sooner, waiting for its unit's
turn, a connection or that `SAVEPOINT`'s answer, it leaves what it sits in as it
was.

## Limits

- **A body consuming or changing an argument its `record_if` reads** is rustc's
  borrow error at the key. Under a `record_if` naming an argument on a method
  run once, the one attempt lends its arguments, for the predicate to read once
  that attempt answered; under one naming none, a function's path or a closure
  over the error alone, the attempt owns them, as with no key. One the body consumes is `E0505`: the body consumes a clone. One
  bound `mut` and changed is `E0502`: bind it plain and change a copy in the
  body. A `&mut` the body writes through is `E0500`: the predicate reads the
  error and the other arguments instead.
- **A method lending its arguments to each attempt is `Send` only where each
  argument it takes by value is `Sync`.** That is every idempotent method, every
  one naming a `fallback`, and one run once under a `record_if` naming an
  argument, for each argument its body only reads. Where the future must be `Send`, a non-`Sync` argument
  taken by value is refused in rustc's words: for an idempotent method as
  `E0277`, the argument "cannot be shared between threads safely", with a note
  pointing at the key; for a method run once that lends under such a
  `record_if` or a `fallback`, as a future that is not `Send`, naming no key.
- **A fallback's future is held as an attempt's is**: in place in a release
  build, boxed in a debug build. clippy's `large_futures` says nothing of a
  decorated method's own `.await`s, whichever of its futures is large; a caller
  awaiting a method whose future is large is still told, at its own `.await`.
- **A fallback has no time past its unit's deadline**: one that waits is cut
  with the unit. Where the attempt given up at that deadline had sent a statement
  in its unit's transaction, its savepoint is rolled back first, a round trip the
  edge cuts the unit in, and the fallback is never asked. One that answers at once
  answers for the unit where no rollback is sent: outside a transaction, or after
  an attempt that sent no statement.
- **A decorated method calling another is two runs, one inside the other's
  attempt.** A refusal the inner policy answers (`Open`, `Full`, `Limited`, or the
  `TimedOut` of its own bound), carried by the outer method's `Err`, is an `Err`
  like any other: its breaker counts it as a failure and an idempotent method sends
  it again, unless its `record_if` and `retry_if` refuse it, as in
  `|error| !matches!(error, PostError::Upstream(e) if e.refusal().is_some())`,
  written for the variant of the error that holds the `ResilienceError`. The
  `Retry-After` an `Open` or a `Limited` carries is waited whole before the call is
  sent again, and one past `BACKOFF_CEILING_MS`, as a fresh opening's is by default,
  ends the run.
- **A policy's breaker times its dependency out at its tightest caller's bound.**
  An inner attempt in flight when the outer policy's timeout cuts the call it runs
  in counts as a timeout in its own breaker: under an outer timeout of two
  seconds, an answer due after three is a failure there, whatever the inner
  timeout. One on a task the call spawned goes to its own bound.
- **A call made inside an attempt of its own policy shares that attempt's slot
  and turn.** A decorated method may call another of its policy, directly or
  through a call under another. Such a call shares the attempt's slot and turn of
  the breaker while no other call does, and gives them back as it ends. The first
  takes the attempt's token. It shares the turn only while the breaker has
  neither opened nor been switched by hand since the attempt was let through:
  once it has, every call the attempt makes meets the breaker as any call does,
  refused `Open` unsent while it is open, so a job looping over a dead partner
  stops sending the moment the breaker opens beside it. The attempt counts once in the breaker: as a failure
  when its own answer fails or a call sharing its turn ended on a failure,
  otherwise as its own answer is judged: a failure a call was sent again past,
  then answered, fails nothing. So a `charge()` that fails after its token helper
  answered counts a failure, a failure propagated counts once, and a probe so
  shaped closes the breaker only if both succeed. The attempt holds its slot
  until it answers, so `CONCURRENCY` bounds its body past the call, and a
  bulkhead of one does not refuse the call. A call made while another shares the
  slot and turn takes a free slot of its own, with its own token and turn,
  refused `Open` beside a probe, and counts its own outcome. Finding no slot
  free, it waits `CONCURRENCY_WAIT_MS` for one or for the attempt's, and with no
  wait, the default, it is refused `Full` at once. One that takes the attempt's
  slot back takes its turn too, which the call before it gave back first: only
  its token is its own, and it counts in the attempt as a call sharing the turn
  does. So twenty pages read inside one `import()` meet `CONCURRENCY` and
  `RATE_LIMIT` as twenty read alone do, and under a bulkhead of one with a wait,
  pages failing in turn count as the import's one failure: `BREAKER_FAILURES`
  counts imports there, not pages. Each is sent under
  its own timeout. Made in an attempt the policy may still send again — an
  `idempotent` one with retries left, or one inside such an attempt — an
  `idempotent` one is sent once, so that two retrying methods do not multiply
  their sends to the dependency. Whether that attempt is sent again is decided
  only once it answers, so where it is not, the call has still been sent only
  once: when its `retry_if` refuses the error the call led to, as the one above
  refuses a `TimedOut`, when its retry is given up past `BACKOFF_CEILING_MS` or
  the deadline, or when it swallows the call's error and answers `Ok`. A method
  whose nested calls must keep their own retries is run once. Made in an attempt
  it never sends again, a method run once or the last attempt, it is sent again
  as its own `RETRIES` say, since nothing around it multiplies them, so a token
  helper answering on its retry leaves the `charge()` around it a success. A call
  spawned onto a task of its own is a call of its own.
- **Arguments drop last declared first**, as the undecorated method's do, however
  the run ends, but for the cases that follow. Of a method run once, under no
  `fallback` and no `record_if` naming an argument, an argument the body reads nowhere and the part
  of a pattern it leaves unbound drop once the attempt has, and one it names under
  a leading `_` drops with the attempt, after the others. The body is read by its
  words there: a local or a struct literal's field spelled as an argument reads as
  it, and can cost rustc's `unused variable` warning on that argument. Run once
  under a `record_if` naming an argument and no `fallback`, an argument the body
  consumes is the attempt's own: one it has not consumed when the attempt ends (failed sooner,
  cut, or refused unsent) drops with the attempt, ahead of the arguments the body
  only reads and in the order the body first names them. A body opening on
  `let _ = (&out, &held);`, its arguments declared `held` then `out`, sets that
  order last declared first. Below a `#[tracing::instrument]`, whose future takes
  the arguments in the order the expansion first names them, they drop last
  declared first too, under every key: the method opens on that statement
  itself where its attempts borrow them.
- **Two decorated calls joined in one unit's transaction take its turn in
  order**: an attempt's savepoint holds the unit until it settles, its call to
  the dependency included, so the other waits inside its own bound and can be
  given up having sent nothing. That timeout is counted against a dependency
  that answered, in its breaker, which can open for every caller. Send them one
  after the other, or outside the transaction.
- **A refusal reaching a tick, a listener or a hook is that unit's error**,
  whichever refused: an open breaker, a timeout, a full bulkhead or a reached
  rate. A tick's, a listener's and a shutdown hook's, in any of the three
  shutdown phases, are filed by their edge — a tick's and a listener's at
  `warn`, a `503` as every edge files one, a tick's at every occurrence while
  the refusal lasts, a hook's at `error` — and a listener's rolls its
  transaction back. An init or bootstrap hook's fails the boot naming the hook, the chain
  in the error `init` returns and on no log line. A refused readiness
  indicator answers down, the chain in a `warn`, and so does one whose attempt
  ends at [its check's
  deadline](/health/indicators/#a-check-under-a-policy), a timeout its breaker
  counts.
- **A decorated `#[liveness]` indicator its policy refuses fails the liveness
  probe**, and the orchestrator restarts the process for a dependency's outage:
  decorate readiness checks, never liveness ones. A refused `#[startup]`
  indicator holds `/health/startup` down, the boot read as unfinished. A
  `fallback` is how any of these units answers instead of failing.
