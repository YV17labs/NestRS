By default every `Err` a decorated method answers is the dependency failing: the
breaker counts it and an idempotent call sends it again. Two keys say otherwise
for the dependency's own answers, and a fallback answers in place of an error. In
`page`, a missing post's `404` is sent once and counted for no failure:

```rust title="crates/features/src/posts/service.rs"
#[resilient(
    upstream,
    idempotent,
    retry_if = |error| !error.missing(),
    record_if = |error| !error.missing()
)]
pub async fn page(&self, post: Uuid) -> Result<String, ResilienceError<UpstreamError>> {
    let url = format!("{}/previews/{post}", self.config.upstream_url);
    let page = reqwest::get(url).await?.error_for_status()?;
    Ok(page.text().await?)
}
```

## What counts as a failure

`retry_if` says which `Err` is sent again, `record_if` which one is a failure in
the breaker, and the two are independent: one says nothing of the other. A `404`
is the upstream's answer, and `page` answers it as
`Err(ResilienceError::Failed(error))` for its caller to read with
`error.missing()` ([the call's error](/fundamentals/resilience/#what-the-client-is-told));
a `503` is attempted again and counted as a failure. An `Err` that `retry_if`
refuses ends the run and is the method's answer.

A predicate reads `self` and the arguments by reference, and `E` is the call's
own error: a timeout is no `E`, so neither `retry_if` nor `record_if` reads it,
and `fallback_if` reads it as `ResilienceError::TimedOut`. A `retry_if`,
`retry_ok_if` or `record_if` that panics fails its attempt as a panic in the body
does: one failure in the breaker, its savepoint rolled back, and the panic
unwinds from the method. `fallback_if` and the fallback are asked once the run
is over, so a panic in either unwinds from the method and fails nothing more.

**An `Err` that `record_if` refuses is counted in neither direction.** It is no
failure and no success either: a closed breaker's window holds nothing of it,
and a half-open breaker's probe answering it hands its turn to the next call.
While half-open, such answers neither close nor reopen the breaker, which stays
half-open, one call at a time, until an `Ok` or a counted `Err` answers.

`retry_ok_if` asks the same of an `Ok`, for a dependency answering "not ready
yet" as a success: one it takes is sent again, and once the retries are spent the
last `Ok` is the method's answer. A timeout is always a failure in the breaker,
and always sent again by an idempotent method.

An error whose chain carries a `Problem` with a `Retry-After` — an upstream's
`429` passed on — holds its retry back at least that long. One asking past
`BACKOFF_CEILING_MS` ends the run.

An answer the dependency gave can also be the call's `Ok` — `Ok(None)` for a
page it does not have, `Ok(Err(declined))` for a card it declined — neither
attempted again nor counted as a failure. That is how a call run by hand, which
reads no key, says it.

## A fallback

`fallback = method` names a method of `Self` that answers in place of the error
a run ends on: the call's own, from its last attempt, or the policy's refusal —
a timeout, an open breaker, a full bulkhead, a reached rate limit. It takes the
decorated method's arguments, in order, then that error by value, and answers
what the method answers; an `Err` it answers is the method's. `card` answers no
card for a refusal, files it at `warn` with its chain, and leaves the upstream's
own failure to its caller:

```rust title="crates/features/src/posts/service.rs"
#[resilient(
    upstream,
    fallback = no_card,
    fallback_if = |error| !matches!(error, ResilienceError::Failed(_))
)]
pub async fn card(&self, post: Uuid) -> Result<Option<String>, ResilienceError<UpstreamError>> {
    let url = format!("{}/previews/{post}", self.config.upstream_url);
    let page = reqwest::get(url).await?.error_for_status()?;
    Ok(Some(page.text().await?))
}

async fn no_card(
    &self,
    _post: Uuid,
    refused: ResilienceError<UpstreamError>,
) -> Result<Option<String>, ResilienceError<UpstreamError>> {
    let error = nest_rs::core::error_message(&refused);
    tracing::warn!(target: crate::posts::TARGET, %error, "link preview refused; answering no card");
    Ok(None)
}
```

`fallback_if` is asked first; left out, every error is answered, the policy's
refusals included, and an error it refuses is the method's answer. The fallback
runs once the run is over, under no policy: no timeout, no retry, counted in no
breaker. `ResilienceError::Unsettled` never reaches it.

Every attempt lends the arguments the fallback is then handed (one under a
`#[cfg]` is handed under that condition, the fallback declaring it under the
same), so the method
binds each to a plain name, none `mut`, takes no `&mut`, and clones what an
attempt consumes, which rustc answers with `E0382` at the fallback's name. A
handler naming its extractor whole, `id: Path<u32>`, takes it apart in its body;
one binding it by a pattern, `Path(id): Path<u32>`, is refused. A fallback taking
other arguments is rustc's `E0308` at the key or `E0061` at the fallback's name,
one naming no method of `Self` is its `E0599`, and one naming the method it
answers for is refused. The fallback is always a method, whose signature is
checked against the decorated one's: a closure or a free function answers
through a method that calls it.

A call run by hand has no key, and its `Result` is its fallback: an
`.or_else(…)` on what `run` answers chooses what stands in for a refusal.

## A unit's transaction

Inside a unit holding a transaction — a mutating route's, a job's — each attempt
of a method declaring `idempotent` or a `fallback` runs in a savepoint of it,
whatever its retries, none included, and so does each attempt of
`run_idempotent`. What the body writes through the data layer is kept only when
the method answers that attempt's `Ok`, and rolled back otherwise, an `Ok` sent
again by `retry_ok_if` included: a failed attempt leaves no write for the next to
repeat, nor beside the error the method answers. The last attempt is rolled back
before a fallback runs, and before the method answers an error `fallback_if`
refused. A method run once with no fallback opens none, nor does any outside a
transaction — a safe route, which runs on the pool: an `Err` there leaves what the
body wrote, as it does with no policy.

A savepoint the database cannot settle ends the method on
`ResilienceError::Unsettled`, attempted no further and handed to no fallback: the
unit cannot commit what the attempt wrote. Another statement of the unit, issued
beside the method, waits until an attempt that sent a statement has settled; a
method's future dropped mid-attempt, once the attempt's savepoint was answered,
leaves its unit unable to commit, as an
[atomic block](/database/transactions/#atomic-blocks) dropped does. Dropped
sooner, waiting for its unit's turn, a connection or that `SAVEPOINT`'s answer,
it leaves the unit as it was.

## Limits

- **A body consuming or changing an argument its `record_if` reads** is rustc's
  borrow error at the key. Under `record_if` on a method run once, the one
  attempt lends its arguments, for the predicate to read once that attempt
  answered. One the body consumes is `E0505`: the body consumes a clone. One
  bound `mut` and changed is `E0502`: bind it plain and change a copy in the
  body. A `&mut` the body writes through is `E0500`: the predicate reads the
  error and the other arguments instead.
- **A method lending its arguments to each attempt is `Send` only where each
  argument it takes by value is `Sync`.** That is every idempotent method, every
  one naming a `fallback`, and one run once under `record_if`, for each argument
  its body only reads. Where the future must be `Send`, a non-`Sync` argument
  taken by value is refused in rustc's words: for an idempotent method as
  `E0277`, the argument "cannot be shared between threads safely", with a note
  pointing at the key; for a method run once that lends under `record_if` or a
  `fallback`, as a future that is not `Send`, naming no key.
- **A fallback's future is held as an attempt's is**: in place in a release
  build, boxed in a debug build. clippy's `large_futures` says nothing of a
  decorated method's own `.await`s, whichever of its futures is large; a caller
  awaiting a method whose future is large is still told, at its own `.await`.
- **A fallback has no time past its unit's deadline**: one that answers at once
  answers for the unit, and one that waits is cut with it.
- **Two calls joined in one transaction wait for each other**: the second's first
  statement waits for the first's whole attempt and may be given up at its timeout,
  counted as the dependency's failure. Send them one after the other, or outside
  the transaction.
- **A refusal reaching a tick, a listener or a hook is that unit's failure.** A
  tick's, a listener's and a shutdown hook's are filed at `error` by their edge,
  a tick's at every occurrence while the breaker is open. An init hook's fails
  the boot naming the hook, the chain in the error `init` returns and on no log
  line. A refused readiness indicator answers down, the chain in a `warn`.
- **A decorated `#[liveness]` indicator refused by an open breaker fails the
  liveness probe**, and the orchestrator restarts the process for a dependency's
  outage: decorate readiness checks, never liveness ones. A `fallback` is how any
of these units answers instead of failing.
