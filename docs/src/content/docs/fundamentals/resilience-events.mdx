`ResiliencePolicy::events()` opens a subscription to what a policy does from
then on, and `next()` waits for each event. This one watches for the breaker
opening of itself:

```rust title="crates/features/src/posts/service.rs"
use nest_rs::resilience::{BreakerPhase, ResilienceEvent};

impl PostsService {
    pub fn watch_upstream(&self) {
        // The task holds the subscription and not the policy, so it ends
        // with the app.
        let mut events = self.upstream.events();
        tokio::spawn(async move {
            while let Some(event) = events.next().await {
                if let ResilienceEvent::Transition { to, manual: false, .. } = event
                    && to == BreakerPhase::Open
                {
                    // The dependency is failing: page whoever is on call.
                }
            }
        });
    }
}
```

## The events

| Event | Told when |
|---|---|
| `Retry` | an attempt answered what the run sends its call again after, told once the retry is certain, before its call is sent: its `attempt`, the first being `1`, the `wait`, and the `error` — its chain, or the timeout's sentence, `None` for an `Ok` sent again |
| `Timeout` | an attempt was given up at its bound, with the `timeout`: zero for a call its unit had no time left to send |
| `Refusal` | the policy refused a call unsent: `ResilienceRefusal::Open`, `Full` or `Limited`, the first and the last with their `retry_after`; `Full` says neither its cause nor a count |
| `Transition` | the breaker moved, `from` and `to` a `BreakerPhase`, and whether by hand (`manual`) |
| `Fallback` | a fallback answers, with the `error` it answers for |
| `Lag` | the subscription fell behind, `missed` events dropped |

A subscription holds up to 256 unread events; past that the oldest are dropped
and `next()` answers a `Lag` before the oldest one kept. Every subscription is
told every event from its opening on, and `next()` answers `None` once the policy
is dropped and every event it told was read. With none open the policy builds no
event. An event names its call by `host` and `method` when a decorated method ran
it, and by neither when `run`, `run_idempotent` or the middleware did: a call
that should be named in its events is a decorated method. An `error` is the
error's chain.

## The reads

What the policy holds now is read on it, each `None` for a mechanism that is off:

| Read | Answers |
|---|---|
| `breaker_state()` | `Closed`, `Open` with its `retry_after`, or `HalfOpen`: what a call arriving now meets |
| `breaker_stats()` | the `outcomes` its window holds and the `failures` among them, and `failure_rate()` from `0.0` to `1.0`; no outcome while open or half-open, the window being forgotten as the breaker opens |
| `concurrency_running()` | the calls holding a slot of the bulkhead, each for its whole run; a call is in this read or the next, never both |
| `concurrency_waiting()` | the calls waiting for a slot |
| `rate_available()` | the tokens of the rate limit free now, none while an attempt waits for one |

## The lines

Every line is on `nest_rs::resilience` and names the policy's `instance`, every
duration in whole milliseconds rounded up. A line about a call adds the `host`
and `method` of the decorated method that ran it, and neither when `run`,
`run_idempotent` or the middleware did. A policy files ten lines:

| Message | Level | Fields |
|---|---|---|
| `attempt failed; retrying the call` | `debug` | `attempt`, `wait_ms`, `error` |
| `attempt answered; retrying the call` | `debug` | `attempt`, `wait_ms` |
| `no time left before the unit's deadline; not attempted again` | `debug` | `wait_ms`, and `error` but after an `Ok` that `retry_ok_if` was to send again |
| `the dependency asked a wait past the backoff's ceiling; not attempted again` | `debug` | `asked_ms`, `ceiling_ms`, `error` |
| `an attempt's savepoint could not be settled; not attempted again` | `debug` | `error` |
| `rate limit reached; waiting for a token` | `debug` | `wait_ms` |
| `rate limit reached; the call is refused` | `debug` | `retry_after_ms` |
| `bulkhead full; the call is refused` | `debug` | `waiters`, the bound met, when every place to wait was taken |
| `the call ended on an error; its fallback answers` | `debug` | `error` |
| `circuit breaker changed state` | `warn` | `state`; an opening by failures adds `failed`, `window`, `open_ms` and `error`, a switch by hand `manual`, and an opening by hand its `open_ms` |

The first is a retry after an `Err` or a timeout — or, through the middleware,
after a status it sends again — and the second a retry of an `Ok` sent again by
`retry_ok_if`, which has no `error`. The third and fourth are a retry left but
not made, with the `error` of the attempt that was to be sent again: the third
has none after an `Ok` `retry_ok_if` was to send again, and the fourth follows
what asks a wait, an `Err` or such a status. Each transition of the breaker files
the last line, probes and switches by hand included, and only an opening by
failures adds the `error` of the failure that opened it. An `error` is the
error's chain with every URL in it cut as [below](#what-a-line-tells-of-a-url),
or the timeout's sentence; the savepoint's line says the chain of the cause the
database could not settle it for.

A call an open breaker refuses, a call its unit had no time left to send and a
run ending on a timeout file no line of their own, in any configuration: the
breaker said it opened once, and each caller is answered its refusal. A call its
unit had no time to send is no attempt: it is told once as a `Timeout` event with
a zero `timeout`. Each mechanism's page says what it files:
[the breaker's](/fundamentals/circuit-breaker/#what-it-files),
[the bulkhead's and the rate's](/fundamentals/bulkhead-and-rate-limit/).

A line carries the error's sentence, so an error quoting a payload value puts it
on the line: keep it out of the error's message, as `UpstreamError` keeps the URL
out ([Resilience](/fundamentals/resilience/#what-the-client-is-told)).

### What a line tells of a URL

An error quoting the address it called would put the caller's path, query or
password on a line, so a URL is read wherever `://` is written and told by its
scheme, its host and a numeric port, and by no byte of a userinfo, a path, a
query or a fragment. Where an `@` follows the end of the authority (a `/`, `?`,
`#` or backslash), the host cannot be told from a userinfo, and the URL is told
by its scheme alone. An address with no `://` is left as written. One limit: a
URL holding a space, a control character, a double quote or an angle bracket
unencoded is read as ending there, so a userinfo holding one is told in part.

### A reqwest error on a line

In a program linking `nest-rs-reqwest`, as the `reqwest` and `authn` features do
and every feature taking `authn`,
a reqwest error that is a link of the chain, reqwest-middleware's included, is
said by what that crate reads of it wherever the framework says an error:

- its kind, in reqwest's words (`error sending request`), and of a status its
  class and its numeric code, `HTTP status server error (503)` — never the URL,
  the reason phrase or a word the dependency wrote;
- beneath it, an I/O error by its OS's sentence or its kind, and a decode failure
  without the value;
- a failure of TLS in rustls's own words, where they quote nothing the peer wrote:
  the alert a peer refusing the handshake sent, by its name (`error sending
  request: received fatal alert: CertificateExpired`), a port that speaks no TLS
  (`received corrupt message of type InvalidContentType`), a peer sharing no
  version or suite with the client;
- a certificate the client refuses, by its constant reason
  (`invalid peer certificate: BadSignature`, `: CaUsedAsEndEntity`,
  `: certificate does not allow extended key usage for server authentication`),
  never its names, its dates or the usages it allows;
- a proxy that opened no tunnel, by one of six constant sentences
  (`tunnel error: proxy authorization required`, `tunnel error: unsuccessful`),
  then the cause beneath it where there is one, said as any other;
- a timeout, a failed lookup, a closed connection, too many redirects and a scheme
  reqwest does not speak, each by a constant sentence;
- a cause another crate's `SafeLink` claims, as that crate says it: a response
  body the policy cut says the policy's own sentence, and an HTTP/2 `GOAWAY` is
  said without its debug data in a program linking `nest-rs-http`.

A cause of any other type is not said: hyper's other reasons, HTTP/2's other
codes, a rejected Encrypted Client Hello and rustls's catch-alls, a serializer's
error, a redirect policy's own. The error is read through an `Arc`, a `Box` or an
`Arc<dyn Error>` around it, and inside an I/O error holding one, one pointer deep:
a pointer to a pointer (`Arc<Box<reqwest::Error>>`) and a reference handed out as
the link are said as `std` displays them, URL included.

An error handing reqwest's sentence on as its own (`#[error(transparent)]`) hides
it from the chain, and without that crate every reqwest error is said as it writes
itself. On the policy's lines and events its URL is then cut to its origin and its
status to its code, its causes said as they write themselves; on an edge's own
line nothing is cut. Name reqwest's error as your error's `#[source]`, as
`UpstreamError` does. A `SafeLink` of your own over `reqwest::Error` refuses the
boot beside that crate's, a type having one sayer: word it your way through an
error type of your own.

## The counters

Once telemetry exports, every policy counts its breaker's transitions, its
retries, the refusals its runs end on and its fallbacks, and observes its reads
as up-down counters: [the instruments](/opentelemetry/metrics/#resilience-policies)
are listed there, each by the policy's `instance`.

## A line, an event and a counter each say their own fact

- A retry's line and event are told once it is certain — as it is decided, or
  once its attempt's savepoint has rolled back — and before its call is sent; its
  counter counts the call once sent again. A retry the policy then refuses unsent
  counts as that refusal.
- A call an open breaker refuses, a call its unit had no time to send and a run
  ending on a timeout are counted as refusals and told as events, and file no
  line.
- A timeout the run retries is told as a `Timeout` event and counted by no
  counter.
- Three timeouts are told by no event and counted by no refusal. Each counts as a
  timeout in the breaker, the dependency having delivered nothing in time, and
  leaves no caller to answer; only a `Transition` follows, when that failure
  opens the breaker:
  - a response of `ResilienceMiddleware` that its caller drops while a read of
    its body has waited past the bound, or that the edge cutting its unit drops
    while a read begun before the unit's deadline still waits;
  - an attempt its caller, or the edge cutting its unit, drops at or past the
    instant its bound ends at, inside a unit whose edge set a deadline, its call
    still unanswered when it is read once more there;
  - an attempt, or such a response while a read of its body waits, dropped by the
    timeout of another policy: the one the method calling it runs under, whose own
    run tells its timeout.
- A refusal is counted and told as its attempt ends, before its savepoint
  settles. A run whose last attempt timed out and whose savepoint then cannot
  settle answers `Unsettled`, its timeout told as a `Timeout` event and counted
  among the refusals all the same. With a retry left to make, that timeout is told
  and counted in the breaker, and among no refusals.
- A transition's line, count and event are told once the breaker's lock is let
  go, in the order the transitions took effect, each once, one thread telling at a
  time. A reader of the breaker, or a switch by hand, from inside the line
  returns. A `Refusal` event can come before the `Transition` that caused it, and
  a subscriber panicking out of the line loses that transition's count and event.
- A `Transition` event says `from`, `to` and `manual`, and not what opened the
  breaker: that is on the `warn` alone. A wait for a token, a retry given up and
  an unsettled savepoint are lines alone.

## With `#[tracing::instrument]`

`#[tracing::instrument]` goes above `#[resilient]` or below, and the order says
what the span covers. Below, one span covers the whole run — every attempt, the
waits between them, what the policy refuses unsent and the fallback — and its
`err` and `ret` record what the method answers. Above, each attempt opens a span
of its own, and `err` and `ret` record what that attempt answered, the
dependency's own error; the fallback runs outside every attempt's span, and the
token `cancel` names is no argument, so no span records it. Above, the decorator
reads the body as `tracing-attributes` 0.1 wraps it, by the `__tracing_` names
that expansion writes, and refuses one wrapped otherwise, saying to write
`#[resilient]` above; a release writing none of those names would be read as no
instrument.

On a GraphQL operation the order says what it says on a route: the instrument
stays on your method. Below `#[resilient]` one span covers the whole run, its
`err` and `ret` recording what the method answers; above it, each attempt opens a
span of its own. The field's guards, gate and pipes run before the method, outside
that span, inside the request's `graphql.field` span.

## Limits

- **A meter provider installed by hand**, with no `OpenTelemetry::init()`, is
  met by a counter's first record and a policy's first run alone: one installed
  after them reports neither. One `OpenTelemetry::init()` seats is met at any
  time, from the next record and the next run.
- **`ResilienceEvents` offers `next()` alone**, no `Stream`.
