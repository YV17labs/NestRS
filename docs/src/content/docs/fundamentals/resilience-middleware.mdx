`ResilienceMiddleware` runs every request your own reqwest client sends under a
[resilience policy](/fundamentals/resilience/). That client is the one you build
for [calling another service](/fundamentals/calling-another-service/), and a
method calling through it takes no `#[resilient]` of its own.

```rust title="crates/features/src/posts/client.rs"
use std::sync::Arc;

use nest_rs::reqwest::ReqwestMiddleware;
use nest_rs::resilience::{ResilienceMiddleware, ResiliencePolicy};

use crate::posts::upstream::Upstream;

pub fn upstream_client(
    policy: Arc<ResiliencePolicy<Upstream>>,
) -> reqwest_middleware::ClientWithMiddleware {
    reqwest_middleware::ClientBuilder::new(reqwest::Client::new())
        .with(ResilienceMiddleware::new(policy))
        .with(ReqwestMiddleware::new())
        .build()
}
```

## Install

```bash frame="terminal"
cargo add nest-rs --features resilience,reqwest
cargo add reqwest@0.13 reqwest-middleware@0.5
```

The second line is there because the client is yours, as [calling another
service](/fundamentals/calling-another-service/#install) says.

## What it sends again

It sends again only a request its method declares idempotent (`GET`, `HEAD`,
`PUT`, `DELETE`, `OPTIONS`, `TRACE`, `QUERY`) or one carrying an
`Idempotency-Key`, and only when its body can be sent twice.

A transport error, a timeout, and a `408`, `429`, `500`, `502`, `503` or `504`
are attempted again and count in the breaker. Any other answer is the
dependency's. A `429`'s or a `503`'s `Retry-After` is waited whole, whether in
seconds or an HTTP-date counted from the answer's own `Date`, so no clock skew
enters. One past `BACKOFF_CEILING_MS` ends the run with that answer. An answer
sent again is dropped unread before the wait for its retry, so no wait holds its
connection or its stream. The last answer comes back as it came; a refusal is a
`reqwest_middleware::Error::Middleware`.

Each request it hands on is one attempt of the policy, and the rate limit, a
token bucket, takes one token for it — the attempt's own, for the first request
sent inside an attempt of the same policy (below). A redirect the client follows is sent inside it ([what an attempt
is](/fundamentals/bulkhead-and-rate-limit/#what-an-attempt-is)).

<Aside type="caution">
The resilience middleware goes before `ReqwestMiddleware`: each attempt is then
a client span of its own, numbered by `http.request.resend_count`. Added after
it, it refuses every request, unsent, with an error saying so.
</Aside>

## What it tells

What it does is told as the policy's own: its [lines](/fundamentals/resilience-events/#the-lines)
on `nest_rs::resilience`, its [events](/fundamentals/resilience-events/) and its counters. Each names the policy's
`instance`, but the default instance's, and none names a host or a method: a request the middleware runs
comes from no decorated method, so two clients sharing a policy are told apart
by nothing it files. Give each client a policy of its own, one instance per
dependency.

A failure rides the retry's line, its event and the breaker's opening as its
chain, and never the request's address: a transport error is said [by reqwest's
kind and the causes that can be
read](/fundamentals/resilience-events/#a-reqwest-error-on-a-line), never a word the
dependency wrote, and any URL another middleware's error quotes [is cut to its
origin](/fundamentals/resilience-events/#what-a-line-tells-of-a-url). A status is
told by its code alone (`the dependency answered 503`), never a header or a body.

## The answer's body

The policy covers the body too, read as it comes and never buffered. The
response is reqwest's own; until its body ends it holds the run's slot of the
bulkhead, the breaker's verdict and what the head left of the attempt's timeout.

That rest is a budget of waiting, shared by every read of the body. A read
counts against it only while it waits for a part that has not come, until the
transport signals one. The time you take between two reads counts for nothing,
so a reader slower than the dependency is never cut.

- Read to its end, the answer counts as its head did.
- A read that fails counts as a failure in the breaker.
- A read still waiting once the budget is spent is given up there, and counts
  as a timeout in the breaker. `bytes()`, `chunk()` and the others answer
  reqwest's error, whose chain holds the `ResilienceError::Refused`, its
  refusal `ResilienceRefusal::TimedOut`, a request
  given up before its head carries, and a line says that cut by the policy's own
  sentence.
- Dropped before its end, the answer counts as its head did. Three cases differ: a
  read you abandoned, in a `select!` or under a timeout of your own, that has
  waited past the budget; a read still waiting when the edge cuts its unit at its
  deadline (a request's, a job's, a health check's), whatever time you took of
  your own before that read; and a read still waiting as another policy's timeout
  drops it, the one its reader runs under. The body is read once more as it is
  dropped: a part that came counts as the head did, a read that fails as a
  failure, and a part the dependency still owes as a timeout. The budget alone
  bounds a read begun at the deadline or after it, and a body handed on past its
  unit, as by a handler answering the response's stream: dropped with that budget
  unspent, the answer counts as its head did.
- A panic while the body is read counts as a failure in the breaker, then
  unwinds on.

A request sent inside an attempt of its own policy, from a `policy.run(…)` or a
method `#[resilient]` with that policy, is a call that attempt makes. It shares
the attempt's slot and turn of the breaker while no other call does, the first
taking its token. It counts in that attempt, once: a failure of its, its body's
included, fails the attempt. A request sent while another shares them takes its
own, as a request sent alone does. Each is sent once: the attempt around it is
what the policy sends again. A body read after the attempt has answered, from a
response handed out of the `run`, holds nothing of the policy.

## Test it

`nest_rs::testing::HttpsPeer` stands in for the upstream: an HTTPS peer on
loopback presenting a `TestAuthority` certificate. It answers each request from
a script, `PeerAnswer::status(503).header("retry-after", "1")` then
`PeerAnswer::status(200)`, and keeps every request's head. A test then asserts
what was sent again and what each attempt carried.

## Limits

- A body whose reads wait for the dependency longer than `TIMEOUT_MS` in all is
  cut there, and the cut counts against the dependency. An event stream idles
  between events, and a slow origin trickles a download: build such a client
  without this middleware, or under a policy of its own.
- The timeout bounds the dependency, never you. A response you keep unread, or
  read slowly, keeps its slot for as long as you take, and a probe's keeps the
  half-open breaker's one turn: read it or drop it. A call refused meanwhile is
  told the probe's whole bound, however long the response has been held.
