Everything a queue holds lives under one prefix, and a job apalis delivers
twice still runs once. This page is the contract behind that sentence: the
keys, the guard that keeps the promise, and what shutdown, delays and an
upgrade from 6.x do to a job in flight.

A queue named `audio`, after one job completed, one dead-lettered and one held
back for an hour:

```text frame="terminal"
$ redis-cli --scan --pattern 'nestrs:queue:audio:*' | sort
nestrs:queue:audio:consumers
nestrs:queue:audio:data
nestrs:queue:audio:data::result
nestrs:queue:audio:dead
nestrs:queue:audio:done
nestrs:queue:audio:failed
nestrs:queue:audio:scheduled
nestrs:queue:audio:settled:01a0de3f-2231-75cf-a401-78dfcb3ecc7d
```

## Where a queue lives

The queue's namespace is `nestrs:queue:<queue>`. apalis, the library the Redis
binding is built on, derives its own structures from it; the framework adds
two of its own beside them, under words apalis does not use.

| Key | Holds | Written by |
|---|---|---|
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:active` | Job ids waiting to be fetched — the list an autoscaler reads | apalis |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:scheduled` | Jobs held back: a delayed push, a retry's next attempt, a job handed back | apalis |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:inflight:<worker>` | Jobs one replica has fetched and not yet settled | apalis |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:data`, `…:dead`, `…:done`, `…:failed` | Records, and the ids of finished, dead-lettered and rescheduled jobs | apalis |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:leases:<job_id>` | Which delivery runs the job right now | the framework |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:settled:<job_id>` | That the job reached its outcome, and which one | the framework |

Nothing sits at the root of the keyspace, so a Redis user whose ACL reaches
`~nestrs:*` and nothing else runs a queue end to end. The framework never reads
or writes apalis's structures except through apalis's own calls.

## At least once, and the guard

Two replicas never fetch the same job from `active`: the fetch claims ids in
one atomic script. A job can still reach a second delivery three ways — a
replica **starting** sweeps every peer's in-flight jobs back onto the queue
(apalis sweeps with a cutoff of *now*, which matches the living too), a
replica whose heartbeat falls behind the orphan threshold has its jobs swept by
a peer, and an acknowledgement can be lost on its way to Redis.

So each delivery takes the job's **lease** before the attempt runs, and renews
it while the attempt runs. When the job reaches its outcome, the delivery
writes the **settled** mark and drops the lease. A second delivery then does
one of two things, and neither runs the job:

- the lease is held elsewhere — the job goes back on the schedule until the
  lease would lapse, and is never acknowledged, so the guard can delay a job
  but not lose one;
- the job is settled — it is acknowledged the way the first delivery answered,
  completed or dead-lettered, without running.

A settled mark lasts an hour, or two orphan thresholds plus a lease when that
is longer; one written while the worker drains lasts a week, since its
acknowledgement may never reach Redis and the next replica may start days
later.

<Aside type="caution">
The guard covers apalis's redeliveries. It cannot make a lease outlive its
holder's silence: a replica cut off from Redis for longer than the lease, and
still running, can see another replica take the job. A handler whose effects
leave the process — an email, a charge — keeps its own idempotency key.
</Aside>

## Replicas, heartbeats and crashes

Each method of each replica consumes under an id of its own — the host name,
then a UUID v7 — so no two replicas share an in-flight set. A replica proves it
is alive every tenth of the orphan threshold, and a peer sweeps its jobs only
once it has missed ten proofs in a row.

A replica that crashes mid-attempt leaves its job in flight. The next replica
to start sweeps it back onto the queue at once and runs it once the dead
replica's lease lapses; with none starting, a running peer sweeps it after the
orphan threshold.

## Shutdown

A shutdown stops fetching at once. Running attempts get the shutdown window,
less a reserve of five seconds (half the window when that is shorter), to
finish where they run. What still runs when the window closes is interrupted
and its job handed back to the queue inside the reserve, so the orchestrator's
`SIGKILL` never lands on a job the worker still holds. A retry's next attempt
is already on the schedule, so it holds nothing up.

## Delayed jobs

The Redis backend declares `Capability::DelayedPush`, so a push can hold a job
back:

```rust title="src/audio/service.rs"
self.queue
    .push(
        AudioQueue,
        TranscodeCommand { file },
        PushOptions::default().with_delay(Duration::from_secs(60)),
    )
    .await?;
```

The job waits on `scheduled`, due on the second its delay ends. Every worker
moves due jobs onto `active` once a second, and so does the producer that
filed them, for as long as it has delayed jobs of its own still ahead — so a
due job appears on the list an autoscaler reads even while no worker runs. A
retry is the same filing: the port's backoff decides when, and the next
attempt waits on the schedule rather than in a worker.

<Aside type="note">
A producer that exits before its delayed jobs are due stops moving them. If
nothing else runs — no worker, no producer pushing delayed jobs to that queue —
they wait on `scheduled`, where a KEDA trigger on `active` cannot see them.
Keep `minReplicaCount: 1` on a queue that takes delayed pushes from short-lived
producers.
</Aside>

## Upgrading from 6.x

6.x handed apalis the queue's bare name, so its jobs sit at the root of the
keyspace. A 7.0 worker serving a queue that still holds jobs there refuses to
start and names them; a 7.0 producer pushing to it warns once. Two ways out:

- **Drain.** Keep a 6.x worker running until the old keys are gone, then
  start the 7.0 workers. Producers can move first: they push under the new
  namespace, which the 6.x worker does not read.
- **Move.** With every worker of the queue stopped, and before anything is
  pushed under the new namespace, rename each of the queue's 6.x keys to the
  same structure under it — `RENAME <queue>:<structure> nestrs:queue:<queue>:<structure>`.
  The in-flight set, named for the queue twice, goes to
  `nestrs:queue:<queue>:inflight:6x`; then
  `ZADD nestrs:queue:<queue>:consumers 0 nestrs:queue:<queue>:inflight:6x`
  lets the first worker to start put its jobs back on the queue.

## Configuration

`RedisWorkerModule::for_root(RedisWorkerConfig { .. })`, or the environment:

| Variable | Default | What it bounds |
|---|---|---|
| `NESTRS_REDIS__WORKER__SHUTDOWN_TIMEOUT_SECS` | `30` | The drain window, hand-backs included |
| `NESTRS_REDIS__WORKER__ORPHAN_AFTER_SECS` | `300` | How long a silent replica keeps its jobs; at least `5` |
| `NESTRS_REDIS__WORKER__LEASE_SECS` | `30` | How long a claim outlives its last renewal; at least `1` |

## Limits

- **Redis Cluster is unsupported.** apalis 0.7's scripts touch several of a
  queue's keys in one call without a shared hash tag, which a cluster refuses.
  A primary with replicas, or a managed single-shard Redis, is the shape.
- **Redis 6.2 or later.** The e2e suite runs on 6.2, 7.0 and 8.6. Before 7.0,
  a worker a starting peer swept fetches nothing until its next heartbeat:
  apalis registers it again on an error text only Redis 7 writes.
- **One fetch per poll.** A method fetches one job every 100 ms per replica,
  so jobs shorter than that run at about ten a second per replica whatever
  their `concurrency`. More replicas is the lever.
- **`failed` only grows.** apalis records there every job it reschedules — a
  retry, a hand-back — and nothing trims it.
