Everything a queue holds lives under one prefix, and a job apalis delivers
twice still runs once. This page is the contract behind that sentence: the keys,
the guard that keeps the promise, what a crash, a Redis timeout and a shutdown
do to a job in flight, and what each capability the backend declares keeps
beside the queue.

The backend runs on [apalis-redis](https://docs.rs/apalis-redis/0.7.4) 0.7.4,
which fetches, schedules and acknowledges jobs. What a job attempt *is* — its
id, its retries, its trace — is the queue port's, and what this page adds to
apalis lives in keys of the framework's own, reached through apalis's calls and
never around them. A queue named `audio`, after one job completed, one
dead-lettered and one held back for an hour:

```text frame="terminal"
$ redis-cli --scan --pattern 'nestrs:queue:audio:*' | sort
nestrs:queue:audio:consumers
nestrs:queue:audio:data
nestrs:queue:audio:data::result
nestrs:queue:audio:dead
nestrs:queue:audio:done
nestrs:queue:audio:failed
nestrs:queue:audio:open:01a0de7d-ceb5-778a-bf40-ac44a74a55c8
nestrs:queue:audio:scheduled
nestrs:queue:audio:settled:01a0de7d-ceb3-73dd-8d85-847b2157a83e
nestrs:queue:audio:settled:01a0de7d-ceb4-75bd-aca2-7ebb6298cccd
```

## Where a queue lives

The queue's namespace is `nestrs:queue:<queue>`. apalis derives its own
structures from it; the framework adds its own beside them, under words apalis
does not use, each keyed by the job id a push returns.

| Key | Holds | Written by |
|---|---|---|
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:active` | Job ids waiting to be fetched — the list an autoscaler reads | apalis |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:scheduled` | Jobs held back: a delayed push, a retry's next attempt, a job handed back | apalis |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:inflight:<worker>` | Jobs one replica has fetched and not yet settled | apalis |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:consumers` | Each replica's in-flight set, scored by its last heartbeat | apalis |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:data`, `…:dead`, `…:done`, `…:failed`, `…:signal` | Records, the ids of finished, dead-lettered and rescheduled jobs, and a wake-up list | apalis |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:open:<job_id>` | That the job was pushed and has not reached its outcome, and the unique key it holds | the framework |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:leases:<job_id>` | Which delivery runs the job right now | the framework |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:settled:<job_id>` | That the job reached its outcome, and which one | the framework |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:cancelled:<job_id>` | That a cancel promised the job never starts | the framework |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:checkpoints:<job_id>` | The progress the job saved | the framework |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:unique:<key>` | The id of the job holding a unique key | the framework |
| `nestrs:queue:<queue>:throttle` | How many attempts of the queue's method started in the current window | the framework |

Nothing sits at the root of the keyspace, so a Redis user whose ACL reaches
`~nestrs:*` and nothing else runs a queue end to end.

## At least once, and the guard

Delivery is **at least once**. Two replicas never fetch the same job from
`active`: the fetch claims ids in one atomic script. A job can still reach a
second delivery three ways — a replica **starting** sweeps up to ten times its
method's `concurrency` of its peers' in-flight jobs back onto the queue (apalis
sweeps with a cutoff of *now*, which matches the living too), a replica whose
heartbeat falls behind the orphan threshold has its jobs swept by a peer, and an
acknowledgement can be lost on its way to Redis.

So each delivery takes the job's **lease** before the attempt runs, and renews
it every third of its length while the attempt runs. When the job reaches its
outcome, the delivery writes the **settled** mark and drops the lease, in one
script. A second delivery then does one of two things, and neither runs the job:

- the lease is held elsewhere — the job goes back on the schedule until the
  lease would lapse, and is never acknowledged, so the guard can delay a job
  but not lose one;
- the job is settled — it is acknowledged the way the first delivery answered,
  completed or dead-lettered, without running.

A settled mark lasts an hour, or two orphan thresholds plus a lease when that
is longer; one written while the worker drains lasts a week, since its
acknowledgement may never reach Redis and the next replica may start days
later.

<Aside type="caution">
The guard covers apalis's redeliveries. It cannot make a lease outlive its
holder's silence: a replica cut off from Redis for longer than the lease, and
still running, can see another replica take the job. A handler whose effects
leave the process — an email, a charge — keeps its own idempotency key.
</Aside>

## Replicas, heartbeats and crashes

Each method of each replica consumes under an id of its own — the host name
when the platform sets one, then a UUID v7 — so no two replicas share an
in-flight set. A replica proves it is alive every tenth of the orphan threshold,
and never more often than once a second, and a peer sweeps its jobs only once it
has been silent
for the whole threshold — ten missed proofs at the default.

A replica that crashes mid-attempt leaves its job in flight. The next replica
to start sweeps it back onto the queue at once and runs it once the dead
replica's lease lapses; with none starting, a running peer sweeps it after the
orphan threshold. The attempt that died is not counted: the job runs again at
the attempt it was at, from the start of its body, so a crash between a
database commit and the settled mark writes twice — the transaction bounds a
retry, never a redelivery.

## When Redis is slow or gone

Every command a worker sends — apalis's and the guard's alike — answers or fails
within `NESTRS_REDIS__CONNECT_TIMEOUT_SECS`, ten seconds by default, so an
outage fails calls rather than holding them. A timeout says the answer did not
arrive in time, never that the command did not run. What a delivery does when a
call fails:

| The call that failed | What happens to the job | The line, on `nest_rs::queue` |
|---|---|---|
| taking the lease | handed back for a second later; never run unguarded | `job lease not asked for; …`, `warn` |
| handing a job back | apalis files its stored record again, due at once — the attempt it was fetched at, or its next one when the filing landed after all — or a sweep does, when Redis refuses apalis too | `job not handed back; …`, `error` |
| renewing the lease | the attempt runs on, and the renewal is tried again | `job lease not renewed; retrying`, `warn` |
| writing the settled mark | the outcome stands; a second delivery could run the job again, and its unique key stays held until its records lapse | `job settled mark not written; …`, `warn` |
| the acknowledgement | delivered again once swept, and answered by its settled mark without running | `job acknowledgement lost; …`, `error` |

A fetch, a heartbeat or a scan of the schedule that fails is retried by apalis
on its next tick, and said at `warn`. A heartbeat that keeps failing past the
orphan threshold lets a peer take the replica's jobs — the case the lease
exists for.

A push, a cancel and a checkpoint's read or save fail within one budget of Redis
going silent too: none sends a second command after one that got no answer, so
the caller reads the connection's cause before the queue port's own net, twenty
seconds, gives up on the call — see
[When a push fails](/queue/producing-jobs/#when-a-push-fails).

**An attempt has no timeout of its own.** A handler that never returns holds its
permit and keeps its lease renewed, so its job is neither lost nor run elsewhere,
and its method runs one attempt fewer at a time until a shutdown interrupts it.
Bound a slow call inside the handler, with `tokio::time::timeout`, and return
the `Err` the retry budget decides on.

## Shutdown

A shutdown stops fetching at once. Running attempts get the shutdown window,
less a reserve of five seconds (half the window when that is shorter), to
finish where they run, and jobs the replica fetched but had not started still
start as permits free inside it. What still runs when the window closes is interrupted
and its job handed back to the queue inside the reserve, so the orchestrator's
`SIGKILL` never lands on a job the worker still holds — give the pod a grace
period above the window. A retry's next attempt is already on the schedule, so
it holds nothing up. An attempt that outlives even the reserve is said at
`error` and left in flight, and runs again elsewhere once its lease lapses.

## Delayed jobs

The Redis backend declares `Capability::DelayedPush`, so a push can hold a job
back:

```rust title="crates/features/src/audio/service.rs"
self.queue
    .push(
        AudioQueue,
        TranscodeCommand { file },
        PushOptions::default().with_delay(Duration::from_secs(60)),
    )
    .await?;
```

The job waits on `scheduled`, due on the second its delay ends — apalis
schedules on whole seconds, and the backend rounds up, so a job never runs
before its instant. Every worker scans the schedule once a second, moving up to
its method's `concurrency` due jobs onto `active` each time, and so does the
producer that filed them — a hundred at a time — for as long as it has delayed
jobs of its own still ahead, so a due job reaches the list an autoscaler reads
even while no worker runs. A retry is the same filing: the port's backoff
decides when, and the next attempt waits on the schedule rather than in a
worker. What that means for a deployment scaled to zero is on
[Concurrency and scaling](/queue/scaling/#on-queue-depth-with-keda).

## Unique keys

A push under a unique key claims `…:unique:<key>` for its job in the same
script that reads it, and opens the job's record, before the job is filed —
so pushes racing for one key queue one job, and a push refused because the
key is held files nothing. The job lets go of the key in the step that settles
it, completed or dead-lettered, and a cancel lets go of it too.

A push can fail after the claim. When Redis answered the filing — refused it
— the job is not on the queue, and the push lets go of the key before it
returns the error. When the answer never came, the job may be on the queue, so
the key stays held by it, and a `warn` naming the job and the key says so:
retrying such a push is refused naming a job the caller never received, and
`cancel_unique` frees the key if that job never started.

## Cancels

A cancel never touches apalis's storage. It writes a tombstone beside the job,
`…:cancelled:<job_id>`, in one script that first checks the job is still
open and that no delivery holds its lease — so `true` is only ever answered
while no attempt runs, and an attempt can never start after it. The same
script closes what the job held: its open record, its unique key and its
checkpoint. The job stays where it waits; the delivery that eventually meets
the tombstone acknowledges it without running, and files
`job delivered after it was cancelled; acknowledged without running`.

## Throttles

A method's throttle is counted in `…:throttle` by the step that admits an
attempt, in the same script as the lease, so every replica counts against one
number and no two admissions race. An attempt over the limit is handed back to
the schedule for when the window ends, as it was fetched, and files
`job deferred to its throttle's next window` as detail. The window's shape, and
what it allows at its edges, is on
[Concurrency and scaling](/queue/scaling/#throttling-starts).

## What a job keeps, and for how long

Every record the framework keeps for a job — its open record, its unique key,
its checkpoint, a cancel's tombstone — lives **a week past the instant the job
is due**, renewed each time a delivery admits the job, files its next attempt
or hands it back. A job that is delivered stays well inside it; the bound is
for a job that never is.

A job can vanish without reaching an outcome a worker sees: a producer that
stops between claiming a key and filing the job leaves the claim behind. That
claim refuses pushes under its key for at most a week past the job's due time,
and `cancel_unique` frees it sooner. The same week caps a job that waits: one
still waiting a week past its due time has been forgotten — its key is free,
its checkpoint gone, and a cancel's promise no longer outlives the tombstone
that kept it.

## Configuration

`RedisWorkerModule::for_root(RedisWorkerConfig { .. })`, or the environment:

| Variable | Default | What it bounds |
|---|---|---|
| `NESTRS_REDIS__WORKER__SHUTDOWN_TIMEOUT_SECS` | `30` | The drain window, hand-backs included; at least `1`, at most `3600` |
| `NESTRS_REDIS__WORKER__ORPHAN_AFTER_SECS` | `300` | How long a silent replica keeps its jobs; at least `5`, at most `86400` — a day |
| `NESTRS_REDIS__WORKER__LEASE_SECS` | `30` | How long a claim outlives its last renewal; at least `1`, and at most half the orphan threshold, so a crashed replica's lease has lapsed by the time a peer takes its jobs |
| `NESTRS_REDIS__WORKER__POLL_INTERVAL_MS` | `100` | How often a method asks for jobs while a permit is free — its ceiling, and its idle cost; at least `10`, and at most the orphan threshold, since the sweep of silent replicas runs on it |

A value out of its bounds fails the boot, whether the environment set it or
`for_root` pinned it, naming the variable — and a lease or a poll too long for
the orphan threshold names both. Every value accepted is one apalis handles:
its sweep converts the orphan threshold with an `unwrap`, which a threshold of
about 262,000 years would panic, and the ceiling of a day keeps it far from
that.

## Limits

- **Redis Cluster is unsupported.** apalis 0.7's scripts touch several of a
  queue's keys in one call — some of them read out of another key rather than
  declared — without a shared hash tag, which a cluster refuses. A primary with
  replicas, or a managed single-shard Redis, is the shape.
- **Redis 6.2 or later.** 7.0's Redis suite passed on 6.2.20, 7.0.15 and 8.6.3,
  and 6.2 is the oldest it ran on. Before 7.0, a worker a starting peer swept
  fetches nothing until its next heartbeat: apalis registers it again on an
  error text only Redis 7 writes.
- **A fetch per poll.** A method starts at most `concurrency` jobs per poll on
  one replica, and a busy replica holds up to `concurrency` jobs it has not
  started — see [Fetching on Redis](/queue/scaling/#fetching-on-redis).
- **A fetch claims 799 jobs at the most.** apalis passes a fetch's ids to
  Redis's Lua in one call, and a sweep ten fetches' worth; Lua takes at most
  7,999 values per call, and a sweep past that loses the jobs it has popped. A
  method whose `concurrency` exceeds 799 still runs that many at once, fetching
  799 per poll.
- **`failed` only grows.** apalis records there every job it reschedules — a
  retry, a hand-back — and nothing trims it.
- **apalis's own attempt cap binds only what 6.x filed.** apalis 0.7 keeps an
  attempt count of its own on every record, raised by each delivery, and kills
  a record answered with a failure once the count reaches the record's cap —
  five by default — with none of the port's events. A hand-back Redis refuses is
  answered that way, so the job is delivered again. Every record 7.0 files — a
  push, a delayed push, a retry, a hand-back — lifts the cap to `u32::MAX`
  deliveries, past any count a job reaches, so apalis's count never ends a job;
  a record a 6.x producer filed keeps the cap of five until a retry or a
  hand-back files it again. The cap is `u32::MAX` and not `usize::MAX` because
  apalis reads it into a `usize`, as wide as the host reading it: a record a
  64-bit replica filed with its own `usize::MAX` would not decode on a 32-bit
  one, and apalis's fetch would fail on every poll for the batch holding it.
- **A settle Redis refuses closes nothing.** The step that marks a job settled
  also lets go of its unique key and closes its open record; when Redis refuses
  it, the worker says so at `warn`, and until the job's records lapse a push under
  its key is refused and a cancel could answer `true` for a job that ran.
