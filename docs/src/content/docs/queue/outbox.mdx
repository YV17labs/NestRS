A push written inside a transaction — a mutating request, a WebSocket message,
a job attempt, a listener's call — is written **into** that transaction. With
the `seaorm` and `queue` features, the job becomes a row of the outbox table,
`nestrs_outbox`, committed or rolled back with the request's own writes; once
the transaction commits, the outbox relay files it on the backend. A rollback
leaves no job behind, a crash after the commit loses none, and the receipt
names a job that exists exactly when the request's writes do. A `push_many`
there is one write, so it lands whole or not at all.

You write the push as anywhere else, and list nothing: `SeaOrmDatabaseModule`
keeps the outbox and attaches the relay. Your migrator lists the framework's
migration, which creates the table — `nestrs g queue` registers it, or
`nestrs g migration nestrs_outbox`, and the boot refuses without it, naming
that remedy:

```rust title="crates/migrations/src/migrator.rs (abridged)"
fn migrations() -> Vec<Box<dyn MigrationTrait>> {
    vec![
        // …
        Box::new(nest_rs::seaorm::queue::OutboxMigration),
    ]
}
```

A push on the pool — a safe request, a `transactional = false` job — is filed
at once, as is one in an app binding no database.

Two answers move from the push to the relay. A unique key another job holds is
checked once the transaction committed: the record is dropped, in one `info`
line on `nest_rs::queue` naming both jobs, and the caller saw `Ok`. A backend
that refuses delays the job without losing it: the relay retries each record on
the job retry backoff, says a failing pass at `warn`, and says once at `error`
when records waited past the stall warning. Delivery stays at least once
([below](#filed-after-the-commit)).

## Filed after the commit

A job pushed inside a transaction waits in the outbox table until that
transaction commits. The relay
then claims committed records with `FOR UPDATE SKIP LOCKED`, so replicas never
share one, files them, and deletes what was filed. A relay that dies between
the push and its delete leaves the record to the next pass, which sends the
same job again: the push script files it once while the queue holds it
([At least once, and the fence](/queue/leases/#at-least-once-and-the-fence)).
On the way down the relay starts no push; the one in flight answers within the
port's 18 s net.
