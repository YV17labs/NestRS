The contract is small: a `#[process]` method returns `anyhow::Result<()>`,
`Err(_)` marks the attempt failed, and `Ok(())` completes the job — once the
attempt's transaction has settled, which is the one way a body that returned
`Ok` can still fail (see
[below](#panics-deserialization-errors-container-misses)). The budget is the
queue port's, the same on every backend: a retryable failure runs again after
a backoff, as the same job, until the budget is spent.

## The basic shape

```rust title="crates/features/src/audio/queue/processor.rs"
#[process(queue = AudioQueue, retries = 3)]
async fn transcode(&self, job: TranscodeCommand) -> Result<()> {
    self.svc.transcode(&job.file).await
}
```

- `Ok(())` ⇒ the attempt's transaction commits and the job is done. If the
  commit cannot be honoured, nothing it wrote landed and the attempt fails
  instead — the one case where `Ok` is not the end.
- `Err(e)` ⇒ the attempt failed. While the budget holds another attempt, the
  job runs again after the backoff below, under the same `job_id` with
  `attempt` one higher. After `retries` failed retries it is dead-lettered
  (see [Where failed jobs go](#where-failed-jobs-go)).
- A **panic** inside the method is caught, logged as
  `job dead-lettered: handler panicked` at `error` with the panic's message,
  and the job dead-letters at once. The attempt's `queue.job` line reports
  `outcome="panic"` beside it, under the same `span_id`. The worker keeps
  running — one bad job doesn't stop the queue's consumer.

The panic is caught by the queue port itself, around the handler, which is
what keeps the event inside the attempt's trace. Each delivery also runs on a
task of its own, and apalis's `CatchPanicLayer` sits outside both as the
backstop for a panic in apalis's own fetch path.

Rust's **default panic hook still prints its own `thread … panicked at …` line
to stderr** first: catching an unwind does not replace the hook. That line
carries no target, so `RUST_LOG` cannot filter it — the framework's own record
is the `job dead-lettered: handler panicked` event beneath it. Install
`std::panic::set_hook` in `main` if you need the raw line gone.

## The retry shape

`retries = N` is `N + 1` attempts in all: the first try and `N` retries. The
default is `0` — the job is tried once. An attempt that never returns counts
too, on a backend that counts the attempts it starts (the Redis one does): a
job whose attempt takes its process down — an abort, an OOM kill, a hang until
the pod is killed — runs `N + 1` times, then is dead-lettered as
`job dead-lettered: retry budget spent by attempts that never returned`.

After attempt `n` fails, the job waits `min(5 min, 1 s · 2^(n − 1))` before
attempt `n + 1`, scaled by a jitter between 0.8 and 1.2. The jitter is derived
from the job's id and `n` rather than drawn, so jobs a shared outage failed
together come back apart, and one job waits the same on every replay:

| After attempt | Wait before jitter | Wait |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 s | 0.8 – 1.2 s |
| 2 | 2 s | 1.6 – 2.4 s |
| 3 | 4 s | 3.2 – 4.8 s |
| 9 | 256 s | 204.8 – 307.2 s |
| 10 and after | 5 min | 4 – 6 min |

The worker says how long it chose, on the line that reports the failure:

```text frame="terminal"
 WARN nest_rs::queue: job failed; will retry within the budget error=transcode failed retry_after_ms=1143 trace_id=01a0deaacf9670f8a924efd95a2ec64b span_id=a83a4d58c89c3d5d
```

On Redis the next attempt is filed on the queue's schedule for when the wait
ends, and the worker that ran the failed attempt moves on: no permit is held
while a job waits, and a shutdown during the wait loses nothing. apalis never
retries on its own; its retry count would be a second budget, invisible to the
method's.

A failure a retry cannot fix is **non-retryable** and dead-letters on the
attempt it happens: a payload that does not deserialize, a pipe rejection, a
missing provider. Every `Err` your method returns is retryable — the budget is
how many times a transient fault gets another chance.

## Resuming where an attempt stopped

A retry replays the whole body. A job that works through a long file can keep
its place instead, with a `Checkpoint<S>` parameter beside the job:

```rust title="crates/features/src/audio/queue/processor.rs"
#[process(queue = AudioQueue, retries = 3, transactional = false)]
async fn transcode(&self, job: TranscodeCommand, mut progress: Checkpoint<TranscodeProgress>) -> Result<()> {
    let first = progress.get().map_or(0, |saved| saved.next_chunk);
    for chunk in first..self.svc.chunks(&job.file).await? {
        self.svc.transcode_chunk(&job.file, chunk).await?;
        progress.save(TranscodeProgress { next_chunk: chunk + 1 }).await?;
    }
    Ok(())
}
```

`get` answers the last state saved for this job — by an earlier attempt, or by
a replica that died holding it — and `save` stores a new one at once. The
state is any `Serialize + DeserializeOwned` value, lasts through the job's
retries and redeliveries, and is cleared when the job completes, dead-letters
or is cancelled.

The method must declare `transactional = false`, and the decorator refuses a
`Checkpoint` without it: a save reaches the backend at once, while a
transactional attempt rolls its database work back when it fails, so the retry
would resume past rows that were undone. A checkpoint needs a backend declaring
`Capability::Checkpoint`; on Redis it is one key beside the job, kept for as
long as the job's other records — see
[Delivery on Redis](/queue/delivery/#what-a-job-keeps-and-for-how-long).

A store that does not answer within the port's net — `BACKEND_TIMEOUT`, twenty
seconds — fails the attempt, retryably: the read before your method runs does
so on its own, and a save does when you return its error with `?`. The clear at
the job's end comes after the attempt, so a store that does not answer it is
said at `warn` and the outcome stands.

## Where failed jobs go

A dead-lettered job's id lands in the queue's `dead` set, and the reason in
its `data::result` hash — both under the queue's namespace:

```bash frame="terminal"
$ redis-cli ZRANGE nestrs:queue:audio:dead 0 -1
01M3FANKWQCV095QBG19STWGT2

$ redis-cli HGET nestrs:queue:audio:data::result 01M3FANKWQCV095QBG19STWGT2
transcode failed
```

The id there is apalis's record id — the job's lines report it as
`backend_id`, beside the `job_id` the push returned. The framework does
**not** ship a dead-letter routing layer: the dead set is the storage's, and
inspecting or replaying it is an operational task.

If you need richer dead-lettering — alerting, replay, archival — catch the
`Err` in the method and republish the payload to a dedicated queue before
returning the error. A `#[processor]` on that queue then runs your
dead-letter logic, and the pattern is the same on any backend:

```rust title="crates/features/src/audio/queue/processor.rs"
#[process(queue = AudioQueue, retries = 3)]
async fn transcode(&self, job: TranscodeCommand) -> Result<()> {
    match self.svc.transcode(&job.file).await {
        Ok(()) => Ok(()),
        Err(e) => {
            self.svc.report_dead(&job, &e).await?;
            Err(e)
        }
    }
}
```

## Panics, deserialization errors, container misses

Four failure modes the framework converts into the same `Err` path,
so you don't have to think about them separately:

- **Panics in the method body** — caught, with a
  `job dead-lettered: handler panicked` event carrying the panic message. The
  job dead-letters immediately (regardless of remaining retries), the worker
  stays up.
- **Deserialization errors** — the macro-emitted `JobHandler` does the
  `serde_json::from_value`. A payload that doesn't match the method's
  job type returns `Err` before your method even runs. Retrying won't
  help: the payload shape is wrong, so it is classified **non-retryable**
  and dead-letters on the **first** attempt, logging
  `job dead-lettered: non-retryable failure` at `error`. The `retries`
  budget is not consumed. The reason names where the payload failed and what
  kind of value it found — ``invalid type: a string, expected u64`` — and never
  the value: serde's own sentence quotes it, and the reason lands in the log
  line and the dead-letter record, where a payload's data does not belong.
- **Container resolution failures** — `Container::get` of the
  `#[processor]` host can fail if a dep wasn't seeded. The handler
  returns `Err` with the resolution error. This usually means a
  configuration mistake at boot, not a transient — investigate, don't
  rely on retries.
- **A transaction that could not be settled** — the body returned `Ok`,
  and the attempt's transaction could not be committed, so nothing it
  wrote landed. The attempt fails rather than reporting writes that were
  never made, and **the database decides whether it is retried** — with
  the answer depending on *where* it failed. A statement inside the
  attempt failed on a serialization conflict, a deadlock, a pool that
  timed out, or a connection the server closed: retryable, because
  nothing is durable before `COMMIT` and the framework therefore knows
  the attempt wrote nothing. A constraint checked at `COMMIT`, or a
  commit whose outcome is unknown because the connection dropped
  mid-flight, is not — it dead-letters at once, because that one may have
  landed and replaying it would write twice. Retrying replays
  the whole body, side effects included, so spending the budget on a
  failure that repeats identically costs several times over and
  dead-letters anyway. See
  [Repo and executor](/database/repo-and-executor/#workers--same-repo-one-transaction-per-attempt).

## Wire-format mismatch

A processor reads only payloads it understands. The wire envelope carries a
version; the macro-emitted handler validates it. A future release that bumps
`WIRE_FORMAT_VERSION` to `2` makes a `v: 1` worker refuse to decode
`v: 2` jobs — fails closed with a clear error rather than
misinterpreting bytes. During a rolling deploy this means jobs queue
up; afterward the new worker drains them.

An envelope with no `id` or `attempt` — what 6.x sealed — still runs, as
attempt 1 under an id minted for its delivery. **Unversioned legacy payloads**
(a bare job JSON, no `v` key) are decoded directly with a `tracing::warn`, so
jobs left from a release before envelopes existed don't get stuck. New
producers always seal.

## Limits

- **The dead set is not routed anywhere.** Nothing alerts, replays or
  archives on your behalf; wire the wrapper above if you need that.
- **A retry replays the whole body.** The transaction bounds what an attempt
  leaves behind in the database, never an email sent or an API called before
  the failure — keep those behind an idempotency key.
- **Delivery is at least once.** A job apalis delivers twice still runs once
  — the delivery guard sees to that — but a replica cut off from Redis for
  longer than its lease can lose the guarantee. See
  [Delivery on Redis](/queue/delivery/).
